Working example, built around Factory Appliance Service's own published facts

A backordered part goes quiet for months, and the only way to find out is another repeat visit

Factory Appliance Service (Tulsa and Oklahoma City, OK, factory-authorized repair for Whirlpool, GE, Maytag, Bosch, Samsung and LG since 2009) repairs in-home major appliances coordinated by phone, with no client-facing way to see where a specific repair order stands. Three BBB complaints filed 2023-2025, all resolved or answered, describe the same shape: a refrigerator needing 6 appointments over 4 months with repeated part replacements before it was fully working, a dryer needing multiple visits with incorrect parts ordered and "no response to date" on a refund until a BBB complaint forced it, and a warranty washer serviced 6 times over 2 months with the customer citing poor phone responsiveness. An independent Yelp review from a different customer describes a freezer technician returning "every couple of months" over 6+ visits for the same unresolved issue. The business's own site has no ticket number, login or status-tracking mechanism of any kind. Below is a working tool sized to close that visibility gap: every repair order gets a committed date for its current milestone, an internal queue sorted by how overdue anything is, and a no-login customer lookup so someone waiting on a backordered part can check real status without calling the office again.

Everything below is fictional. Customer names, order details and dates on this page are sample data, illustrative only, not Factory Appliance Service's real client or work-order records. Two sample orders below are deliberately shaped to mirror the pattern described in the business's own public BBB complaints and an independent Yelp review (a part-ordered repair going months without a proactive update) to make the tool concrete, not because they reflect a real Factory Appliance Service customer or order. This tool addresses the visibility gap, not the underlying diagnostic or parts-sourcing difficulty itself, which is outside any office tool's control.

How this gets built for real

The work order number is whatever Factory Appliance Service already uses. A real build reads from the existing service-ticket number instead of inventing a new one for the customer to remember.
Committed dates here are entered by whoever logs the order. A real build can pull the parts-supplier ETA straight from the distributor's own confirmation and let the office update it the moment the supplier reports a change, instead of the customer finding out by calling.
This does not fix a hard diagnosis or a wrong part getting ordered. Two of the three BBB complaints this offer is grounded in involved parts or diagnosis issues, a separate, harder operational problem outside this tool's scope. What this closes is the silence around a real delay, so a customer sees an honest, current status instead of nothing at all until they call again.
