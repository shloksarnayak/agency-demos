Ware Landscaping & Snow Removal: four coordination gaps, made into tracked records

Public complaint records for this company describe a recurring pattern: a snow-removal bill disputed over whether the contractual trigger condition was actually met, a final invoice with a line item the customer says was never requested, a dig job that severed a customer's internet cable because utility-locate coordination wasn't tracked ahead of time, and a repeated theme of promised callbacks that never happened. This example shows the same four small logs from the general template, tuned to this company's own contract structure and the specific gaps its complaint record points to.

Direct fetches of the company's own live site (homepage, /contact/, /about/, /careers/, including its most detail-rich page for prospective hires) show no online booking widget, no customer portal, and no CRM-linked invoicing or dispatch software mentioned anywhere. Contact is phone plus a bare quote-request form. That absence lines up with the complaint pattern above. The company carries an overall A+ BBB accredited rating and is generally well reviewed (4.6/5 across 379 Birdeye reviews), so this is framed as a real-but-minority coordination gap at a company operating at real capacity across 4 offices, not a business-wide crisis. None of this is presented as proof of intent; it is the documented pattern the tool below is built to prevent going forward, by making the trigger condition, the locate confirmation, the invoice scope, and the promised callback all visible before a dispute happens.

How this gets built for real

The threshold check is a simple comparison here. A real build can pull measured snow/ice conditions from a weather-data API keyed to each property's address, so office staff don't have to enter it by hand.
The locate gate is a manual confirmation step in this demo. A real build can require an uploaded locate-ticket number or photo before the status flips, for a stronger audit trail.
The invoice match is a description comparison. A real build ties it to whatever quoting/invoicing tool the company already uses, so it never depends on retyped line items.
