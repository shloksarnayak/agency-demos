A reported defect should get a case number, not a verbal promise

Stone Tech Fabrication's own site (Process, Designers & Builders, and Warranty & Aftercare pages) shows a genuinely sophisticated pre-install and install-day process: 3D laser templating, full 3D rendering with customer sign-off before any stone is cut, and a live crew-tracking link on installation day. The one gap that process doesn't cover is what happens after a defect is reported. The site's own Warranty & Aftercare section is general reassurance with no case number, no stated response time, and no visible status. Stone Tech's BBB file shows what that gap looks like in practice: one 2024 complaint describing an incomplete cut, a broken piece, a promised callback that never came, and a second complaint BBB itself marks unanswered. This example adds exactly one thing behind that existing process: the moment a defect is reported, it becomes a ticket with a timestamp, a named owner, and a stated response window, and if nobody logs a response in time, it escalates automatically instead of going quiet.

How this maps to Stone Tech Fabrication specifically

It sits behind the existing Warranty & Aftercare path, not instead of it. Stone Tech's own site already tells a customer to reach out if something's wrong; this tool starts the clock the moment that happens, rather than replacing the contact method itself.
The 48-hour response window and the two owners shown here are illustrative. A real build uses whatever response time Stone Tech actually wants to commit to, and whichever role should be the second line, the shop manager named in the BBB complaint response, or ownership directly.
Sample ticket STF-1002 models the documented pattern. The 2024 BBB complaint describes a cut piece, a broken piece, and a promised callback that never came; that sample ticket shows exactly that: no response logged, and the window closing without anyone having followed up, so it escalates instead of going quiet the way the real complaint did.
Escalation here is a visible flag and a log entry, nothing is actually sent. A real build would text or email the second owner the moment the window closes, giving Stone Tech a chance to catch a stalled case before it becomes a second unanswered BBB complaint.
Deliberately out of scope: 3D rendering, laser templating, and live crew tracking. Those are Stone Tech's own existing pre-install and install-day tools, confirmed via their own Process and Locations pages. This tracker only covers what happens after a defect is reported, where their own site has no equivalent system today.
