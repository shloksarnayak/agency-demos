FFixpoint Studio Templates / Booking & service-ticket portal
Template, not built for a specific business

Let a customer self-book a visit, then see a real ticket instead of calling to check

Businesses that handle every appointment and every "what's going on with my service" question by phone tend to develop two related gaps: a prospective or existing customer has no way to see open time slots and claim one, so they call and wait on hold or leave a message; and once something goes wrong, the only way to check status, who is assigned, and what it will actually cost is another phone call, often a repeat one. This page shows two small, working tools that close both gaps: a real time-slot booking calendar, and a service ticket that shows an assigned technician, an ETA, and a price that has to be confirmed in writing before anything can be marked billed.

Everything below is fictional. When this gets built for a real business, the calendar reads from whatever already tracks the team's real availability, and the ticket tracker sits in front of whatever system already handles billing, it does not replace either one.

1. Self-service booking calendar

Pick a visit type and a day, then claim an open slot. No phone call, no waiting for a callback to find out what's available.

Hours and days shown are illustrative placeholders for this template. A real build uses the business's own published hours.

2. Service ticket & price-confirmation tracker

Every ticket shows its real stage, the technician assigned, and an ETA. A ticket cannot be marked billed until a price has been confirmed in writing here, visible to the customer before any charge posts.

How this gets built for real

The calendar is a standalone log here. A real build reads and writes whatever calendar or scheduling system the team already uses, so staff see one schedule, not a second one to keep in sync.
Booking confirmation is on-screen and in local storage only. A real build sends an actual confirmation email or text, and can sync to the business's own calendar.
The price-confirmation gate is a field, not a payment system. It only needs to record one thing: has the customer been given a written price, and what was it, so that number is checkable if a dispute ever comes up.
See it built for a real business: Infinity Water Systems, Mitchell Roofing Company, or AMB Driving School.
