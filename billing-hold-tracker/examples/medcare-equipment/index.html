MedCare Equipment: the moment a pickup happens, billing should already know

MedCare's own BBB complaint file shows the same shape repeated across multiple separate cases: equipment is picked up or returned, but that fact never reaches billing, and the patient keeps getting charged until they escalate. In one documented case a hospital bed was picked up by another company and MedCare kept billing for six months, and when the family cited call logs proving they had reported it, MedCare's own note reads that an "IT search did not return any calls in system records." This example shows a small tool that gives every pickup and return a timestamped record the moment it happens, and automatically raises a billing hold until someone in billing clears it.

Several of the documented complaints share the same root cause: a CPAP mask billed $23.16 after the equipment was returned and a release was signed, a CPM machine billed through August 5 despite being picked up on July 22 (refunded $240 only after a BBB complaint), and a patient's account wrongly charged $55 in copays because it was not flagged correctly. MedCare's IT team has already invested in point tools for other parts of the operation (a new phone system and fleet-dispatch software, per those vendors' own case studies), and patients are routed across at least four separate portals for different purposes, but nothing documented ties a warehouse pickup event directly to a stop on recurring billing.

How this gets built for real

This is a bridge, not a core-system replacement. MedCare already runs point tools for phones and fleet dispatch; a real build would push one signal, stop billing this patient for this equipment, into whichever billing system currently runs recurring charges, rather than replacing it.
The field-log form has to work from a truck or a warehouse floor. A real build would let a serial or barcode scan fill in most of the form instead of typing, so it actually gets used at the moment of pickup.
Clearing a hold stays a deliberate billing decision. The tool raises the flag the instant a pickup or return is logged; a person in billing still confirms and clears it, matching how the refunds in the complaint record were ultimately resolved by a person, just much later than they should have been.
