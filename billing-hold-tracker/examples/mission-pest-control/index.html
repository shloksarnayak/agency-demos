Mission Pest Control: the moment a cancellation email lands, billing should see it too

Mission Pest Control's own public BBB complaint file shows the same shape repeated across several separate cases spanning late 2023 through late 2025: a customer emails or calls to cancel, the request is acknowledged in writing or logged with an account manager, and the account is billed again the following month anyway. This example shows a small tool that gives every cancellation request a timestamped receipt the moment it comes in, and automatically raises a billing hold until someone confirms the account is actually closed.

Of the complaints published on Mission Pest Control's BBB profile, several independently describe the same mechanism: a cancellation request goes in by email or phone, an account manager acknowledges it or promises to process it, and the card gets charged again the following billing cycle regardless. Dated examples include a customer cancelled by email in October 2025 whose card was still charged the next month; a customer who cancelled in December 2024 but kept being billed until May 2025 despite repeated promises from an account manager; and a customer who cancelled in September 2023 and was still being charged four months later, with calls to the account manager going to voicemail. Mission already runs a FieldRoutes customer portal (confirmed live at mission.pestportals.com), so the account infrastructure exists, what's missing is a receipt that a cancellation request was actually received and a hold that stops the next charge until someone confirms it. This tool is scoped narrowly to that acknowledgment gap, not the phone-based retention conversation itself, which may be a deliberate save step this pitch isn't targeting.

How this gets built for real

This bridges the front office to FieldRoutes, it doesn't replace it. Mission already runs FieldRoutes for scheduling and account management; a real build would push one signal, a cancellation request was received, into the existing portal so billing sees it the same day.
The receipt is the whole point, not the retention step. This deliberately stays out of the phone-based save conversation an account manager may run, it only guarantees a written cancellation request gets an instant, logged acknowledgment.
Clearing a hold stays a deliberate billing decision. The tool raises the flag the instant a cancellation is logged; a person in billing still confirms and clears it, the same way the BBB complaints were ultimately resolved by a person, months later than they should have been.
