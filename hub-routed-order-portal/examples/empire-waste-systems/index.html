Working example, built for Empire Waste Systems

Order a container online, routed to the right hub automatically

Empire Waste Systems' own site says it plainly: "Old-School Service. We Pick Up The Phone." Every order today, across three service regions and two separate hub phone lines, still runs through a call or a wait-for-callback lead form. Local competitors in the same Reno/Sparks/Carson City market (Trash Daddy Dumpsters, Sierra Dumpsters, Junk King Reno, Trash Panda) already advertise online booking. This prototype shows what an online order form looks like without giving up the phone relationship: pick a service area, the request routes itself to whichever hub actually covers it, and a repeat contractor account can see its own order history instead of calling to ask.

Grounded in what's actually published

The gap, in Empire Waste's own words. The order page is a callback lead form (name, email, address, zip, phone, size dropdown, notes, terms), ending with "One of our staff members will contact you to complete your order." The site frames this as deliberate: "Old-School Service. We Pick Up The Phone." Independently checked, both the homepage and the order page, no booking, login, or tracking technology exists anywhere on the site today.
Two real hubs, coordinated by phone. 775-297-3483 covers the Reno/Sparks/Carson City side, 775-831-2246 covers the Lake Tahoe/Incline Village side, both listed on Empire's own site. The area-to-hub grouping used on this page groups each of Empire's own published service areas by which side of that split it geographically falls on. That grouping is this prototype's own inference, not a routing table we've seen (none is published), and should be confirmed with Empire before anything like it goes live.
Same four sizes Empire already offers. 10, 20, 30 and 40 yard are the exact options on Empire's own order-page size dropdown, independently checked. Nothing new is being introduced, this only moves the same choice online.
Local competitors already offer this. Trash Daddy Dumpsters, Sierra Dumpsters, Junk King Reno and Trash Panda, all independently found serving the same Reno/Sparks/Carson City market, already advertise online booking. This isn't a hypothetical industry trend, it's the specific competitive reality of Empire's own backyard.
Not a replacement for "we pick up the phone." Empire's 4.9/5 Google rating and repeatedly-named staff (Michelle, Travis, Pete) in reviews suggest the phone-first relationship works and customers like it. This is meant as an additive option for repeat contractor customers who want to reorder without waiting for a callback, not a replacement for the phone line.
What a real build adds. A production version would connect to whichever system Empire's staff actually use to log and dispatch orders today (or replace the callback form directly), add real account authentication per contractor customer, and confirm the hub-routing grouping above with Empire rather than relying on this prototype's own geographic inference.
