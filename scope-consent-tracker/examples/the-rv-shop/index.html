A fee you see before the truck rolls, and an insurance claim that never goes out without a yes first

The RV Shop's own site has a request-an-appointment form and nothing else customer-facing: no scope sign-off, no claim-authorization step, no record of what a customer agreed to. This page shows a small tool that adds exactly those two gates to an existing repair ticket: the trip fee and hourly rate disclosed and acknowledged before diagnosis starts, and any insurance claim held until the customer explicitly approves it, with a timestamped record for every one of those decisions.

How this gets built for real

Two separate gates, on purpose. The initial diagnosis estimate (signed up front) and anything found after the job starts are tracked as different events with different timestamps, so "the invoice grew" and "the customer never agreed to that" are no longer the same question.
The insurance-claim gate blocks the action, it does not just log it. In this demo a claim cannot be marked submitted until the customer approves it here; declining routes the job to self-pay instead. A real build ties this to whatever claim-filing step the shop's own insurance workflow already uses.
"Approved" here is a click and a timestamp. A real build can require a typed name or an e-signature provider on the insurance-claim gate specifically, since that is the higher-stakes decision; this demo shows the workflow, not a specific vendor.
The repair categories mirror what The RV Shop itself advertises (mechanical, electrical, plumbing, appliance, AC, generator, awning, roof, slide-out/weather-seam, and body-and-collision repair), taken from the company's own site; the sample rates above illustrate the $350/hr figure found in public reviews, not a confirmed internal price list.
