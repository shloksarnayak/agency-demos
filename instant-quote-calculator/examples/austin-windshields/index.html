What I'd flag before this goes live

The VIN decode is real, the pricing isn't. The year/make/model/body style returned above comes from NHTSA's live public VIN database — that part works against any real VIN today. The dollar figures are marked "Illustrative" throughout because your site doesn't publish a rate table; a real build needs your actual repair, replacement, and recalibration numbers by vehicle class.
ADAS recalibration is suggested by model year, not confirmed by the VIN. NHTSA's data on which specific trims carry a forward-facing camera is inconsistent across manufacturers, so this flags "likely needs recalibration" for 2018+ vehicles as a starting point and leaves the final call to whoever's writing the ticket, the same way a tech would visually confirm it today.
Service-city check is a name match against your published list, not a real address lookup. It confirms the city the customer typed is one you already serve; it doesn't validate a street address or calculate drive time.
The text thread is a scripted simulation, not a working phone number. Standing this up for real means a provider like Twilio wired to a triage script and, ideally, this same VIN/quote logic so the auto-reply carries a real number, not just "we'll call you back."
This calculates and demonstrates, it doesn't book anything. No calendar, no payment, no confirmation email. The honest next step after "here's your estimate" is still your existing quote process — this just gets someone there already knowing what to expect, at 9 PM on a Saturday instead of Monday morning.
