The company's public BBB profile shows 5 complaints over the last 3 years, including two separate no-show incidents (11/14/2024, 11/24/2025) where dispatch staff repeatedly told a waiting customer the technician was "en route" or "30 minutes away" over more than two hours with no actual arrival, a pattern independently echoed in an Angi review of the same November 2025 date. A separate BBB response from the company itself (05/17/2026) admits "an automated reminder system" was "mistakenly triggered," sending a collection notice to a customer who had declined a $1,600 estimate for work already done. Source: Attic Pros, Inc.'s public BBB complaint profile.

Direct fetches of the company's own live site (homepage, /about-us/, /careers/, /faqs/, /our-projects/, /reviews/) plus attempted /contact/ and /contact-us/ pages (both returned 404) show the only customer-facing intake tools are a phone line and a "Book Free Estimate" form, no online scheduling, no technician-tracking page, and no customer account or portal. The company is genuinely well reviewed overall (4.8/5 across 1,900+ reviews on its own site, A+ accredited BBB, Diamond Certified), so this is framed as a real-but-minority coordination gap at a company operating at real scale across 3 offices, not a business in crisis. None of this is presented as proof of intent; it is the documented pattern the tools below are built to prevent going forward, by making the technician's real status and the account's real billing status both visible before a dispute happens.

1. Dispatch & appointment-window board

Directly targets the two BBB no-show complaints. Dispatch staff can only relay a technician's actual logged status, and a scheduled window that closes with no arrival logged is flagged automatically instead of staying a verbal reassurance.

2. Automated billing-reminder audit & hold

Directly targets the company's own admitted "mistakenly triggered" automated reminder and the second complaint describing a final notice sent after cancellation. Marking an account declined or canceled blocks every reminder tied to it immediately, before it can fire.

How this gets built for real

The dispatch board is a standalone log in this demo. A real build reads and writes whatever system Attic Pros' own dispatchers already use to schedule the three-office job calendar, so staff have one screen, not a second one to keep updated.
The window-passed flag is a simple time comparison here. A real build can also push an internal alert the moment a window closes with no arrival logged, so the office knows before the customer calls.
The billing guard is a gate in front of the existing automated reminder system, not a replacement for it. It only needs one signal per account: hold or release, which is exactly the check that was missing when the "mistakenly triggered" reminder went out.
