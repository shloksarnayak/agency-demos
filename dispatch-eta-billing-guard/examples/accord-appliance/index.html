Accord Appliance: a technician's arrival gets logged, not just claimed

Accord Appliance's public BBB complaint record includes an April complaint describing a customer who stayed home all day waiting for a technician, was later told the technician had arrived at 1:25pm and found nobody there, and was marked as a no-show, while the customer's own Ring doorbell camera footage showed no technician ever came to the door. That single dispute sits inside a larger pattern: 33 BBB complaints over three years, 8 closed in just the last twelve months, with missed appointment windows and disputed no-shows recurring throughout. This example shows one small, working tool built directly around that gap: a technician's arrival has to be logged with a verification step at the time it happens, not asserted afterward, so a "we were there" claim and a "nobody came" claim don't have to be settled by whoever argues harder on the phone.

This is framed as the documented pattern the tool below is built to prevent going forward, not as a claim about any single visit. The fix isn't "trust the technician more" or "trust the customer more," it's removing the gap where an arrival can be recorded as fact with nothing behind it: every arrival now requires a logged verification step at the time it happens, and a window that closes without one is flagged automatically instead of quietly defaulting to "customer no-show."

Arrival-verification & no-show dispute board

A technician can be marked "arrived" only by attaching a verification step (a code texted to the customer, a photo of the unit, or a signature). If a scheduled window closes with no verified arrival logged, the job flags automatically as unverified instead of silently becoming a no-show. If a customer disputes a visit, the tool checks the record: a dispute against a job with no verified arrival gets flagged for review rather than closed against the customer.

How this gets built for real

Verification here is a typed note; a real build ties it to something harder to fake. A code texted to the customer's phone at check-in, a geotagged photo, or a signature capture on the technician's own device, whichever fits how Accord's technicians already work in the field.
The window-passed flag is a simple time comparison. A real build can push that flag straight to a dispatcher's screen the moment it happens, not just show it here, so office staff know before the customer calls.
This sits in front of whatever already schedules jobs. It reads and writes the same job calendar dispatch already uses; it does not replace it, it just makes sure "arrived" always means something was actually logged.
