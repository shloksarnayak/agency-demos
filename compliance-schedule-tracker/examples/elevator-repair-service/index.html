One building, several units, several clocks, plus a phone that never stops

ERS maintains, inspects, modernizes, and installs commercial and industrial elevators, escalators, moving walkways, material lifts, and rack-and-pinion units across Houston, all through a phone line, a generic "Request Service" contact form, and email today, with the company's own site setting the expectation of a callback "within 24 hours." A single client building can carry several pieces of equipment at once, each on its own preventative-maintenance clock and its own separately-tracked state inspection clock, while emergency calls, repairs, and modernization jobs all still need to be routed somewhere. This page shows what a per-building due-date board plus a shared service queue, with a self-service status check for building managers, could look like, using fictional sample accounts.

Client building compliance board (fictional sample accounts)

Each building can carry more than one piece of equipment, and each piece keeps two independent clocks: its next preventative-maintenance visit and its next state-mandated inspection. One clock going overdue never masks the other, and logging a completed visit or inspection updates that unit's own record immediately.

Why this, specifically, for ERS

Matches the pain in ERS's own words. ERS's own site routes every preventative-maintenance visit, emergency repair, state inspection, and modernization or installation request through the same phone call, the same generic "Request Service" contact form, or email, and sets the expectation of a callback "within 24 hours." A building manager with an urgent question today has no way to check status without waiting on that callback.
One building, several units, several clocks. A single client building can run a traction elevator, an escalator, and a material lift on three different preventative-maintenance schedules, each also carrying its own separate state inspection clock. The sample accounts below model that shape, one building, multiple equipment records, each with two independently tracked due dates, neither hidden behind the other.
Covers all five job types ERS lists, not only inspections. The service queue tracks preventative-maintenance visits, state-mandated inspections, emergency repairs, and modernization or installation jobs in one shared view instead of relying entirely on phone calls and a generic form, with anything sitting too long in a stage flagged automatically.
