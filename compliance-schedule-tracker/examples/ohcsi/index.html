A single client site can run several pieces of equipment at once, each due for its next OSHA/ASME periodic inspection on its own schedule. This page shows what a due-date board that tracks every piece of equipment per site, not one blended reminder, could look like, using fictional sample accounts.

Client site compliance board (fictional sample accounts)

Each site can carry more than one piece of equipment. Every piece keeps its own last-inspection date, its own service classification, and its own computed due date, one going overdue never masks another on the same site.

New site / equipment record

Fills the role of the phone call or contact-form message OHCSI gets today. Type an existing site name to add a second piece of equipment to that same site, or a new name to start one. The inspection interval and next due date are computed automatically from the equipment type and service classification, not typed in by hand.

Why this, specifically, for OHCSI

Matches a current, first-party pain signal. OHCSI's own employment page currently lists an active Service Manager opening whose stated responsibilities include "assist with scheduling service work, including annual crane inspections and emergency repairs," alongside hiring at least three more technicians. That's a present-tense admission that scheduling load is a live operational strain, not an inferred gap. This board is the tool that supports whoever takes that scheduling role, it doesn't replace them.
One site, several cranes and hoists, several clocks. An industrial client can run a bridge crane, a jib crane, and an overhead hoist on three different inspection classifications at once. The sample accounts below model that shape, one site, multiple equipment records, each with its own independently tracked due date, none hidden behind whichever one gets checked most often.
Extends what OHCSI already produces. OHCSI's own site already promises "Easy to Read and Understand Inspection Reports" to clients. A client-facing due-date view of that same report history is a natural next step from work the company already does internally, not a new service line.
No visible complaint here, and that's stated plainly. OHCSI is BBB Accredited with an A+ rating and zero complaints on file. This isn't pitched as fixing something broken, it's what lets a well-run shop keep pace with the growth its own hiring posting describes, three years shy of a quarter-century in business.
Larger competitors already run this digitally. Konecranes operates "yourkonecranes.com," an online customer portal marketed as giving clients "crane maintenance information online any time," including service history and inspection records, exactly the kind of self-service layer OHCSI's own site has none of today.
