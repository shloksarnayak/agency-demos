Midwest Boat Service: catch a duplicate storage plan before it bills twice

Midwest Boat Service's own BBB complaint file documents a customer who was accidentally set up on two simultaneous storage billing plans at once, resulting in 15 charges over 8 months instead of 8, only resolved after a credit-card dispute over the roughly $199.50 still owed after partial refunds. A separate BBB customer review from February 2026 describes a pattern of unauthorized charges from November 2025 to February 2026, serious enough that the reviewer referenced a police report, which the business's own response called an honest mistake that was refunded in full. A direct fetch of the live site found WordPress with the BookingPress plugin for one-off service appointments and Square/PayPal for payment, but nothing that tracks recurring storage plans or lets a customer see their own billing. This example shows a small tool that closes both gaps: starting a new storage plan checks for an existing active one first, and a no-login customer portal lets an account holder see every plan and charge and flag one they do not recognize.

How this gets built for real

The duplicate check has to run against the real active-plan list. A real build ties this into whatever tracks Midwest's actual storage customers today, not a separate spreadsheet, so the check is live at the moment staff try to start a plan.
The customer portal reads billing, it does not decide it. Flagging a charge raises it for a person in the office to review, matching how both documented complaints were ultimately resolved by a person, just much later than they should have been.
This sits alongside BookingPress and Square/PayPal, it does not replace either. One-off service appointments keep working exactly as they do today, this only adds the missing piece: recurring storage billing with a duplicate check and a customer-visible record.
