Working example, built for KA RV Repair

Right now, checking on an RV repair means calling and hoping someone picks up

I checked karvrepair.com's homepage, Contact Us and Schedule Service pages directly. The only intake tool present is a one-way Contact Form 7 form, no booking system, no CRM, no work-order status page, and no signature of a shop-management platform anywhere in the page source. With two shop locations (DeBary and Fruitland Park) plus mobile technicians in the field, that means the only way a customer learns where their RV stands is calling and hoping the right person picks up, and BBB's own reviews page for the business describes exactly that gap.

What's real: BBB's customer-reviews page for KA RV Repair LLC (fetched directly, 4 of 5 reviews one-star) documents a customer who asked for management involvement "no less than 8 times" over a 90+ day repair, a 3-month delay replacing an auto-leveling jack, and a separate 90+ day slide-adjustment and cable-repair job. General web-reviews independently describe the same shape: "I asked to have Andrea call me he never did" and "I contacted KA RV requesting a call from Andre (owner). He did not, and has not, returned my call." (The business's own About Us page spells the owner's name Andrey; reviewers spell it a few different ways, noted here rather than smoothed over.) A third source, ComplaintsBoard's KA RV Repair page, showed a corroborating title in search results but returned HTTP 410 on direct fetch, so it is flagged as unverified, not counted as a confirmed third source.

What's illustrative: the sample tickets below are fictional, not real customers or RVs. What I'd flag before this goes further

Only the four sample reference codes above return anything. This isn't connected to a real work order, so it can't look up an actual customer's RV. A real build would read from whatever intake record each stage change is logged against, likely the existing Contact Form 7 submission plus a simple shared spreadsheet or Airtable base staff at both locations update.
Automatic SMS/email pings are simulated as an internal log entry here, not actually sent. A real build wires each stage change to an SMS or email provider so the customer update goes out the moment a technician marks the change, at either shop or in the field, instead of relying on someone remembering to call.
The five stages are the scoped proposal, not a copy of an internal process KA RV already runs, since the direct site check found no existing status system to model this against. Worth a short conversation to confirm these match how the shop actually talks about a job's progress.
The verified email, info@karvrepair.com, was found in the site's own structured data (schema.org JSON-LD) on the homepage and contact page, not as a visible mailto link in the rendered page. Noted plainly rather than glossed over.
