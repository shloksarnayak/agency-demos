Working example, built for Phillip McCallen Motorcycles

Once a bike is booked in, or an aftersales issue is open, the only way to get an answer is another phone call

Phillip McCallen Motorcycles (Lisburn, Northern Ireland) is a multi-brand dealer, authorized for Triumph, Kawasaki and KTM new and used plus used Ducati, Honda, Husqvarna, Suzuki and Yamaha, with its own in-house workshop, parts counter, clothing, e-bikes and a riding academy. Five independently dated Trustpilot reviewers, spanning 2021 to 2026, describe the same gap on the workshop side: a promised callback that never comes. John Wilson (22 Feb 2024): "I would be called back the same day or the next day to arrange a date to have it brought in... It is now one year later and I'm still waiting for that call back." Jim B (28 Aug 2021): "Theres been a big delay on getting a bike booked in for a service or any issue for over a year." John Hughes (10 Jul 2021): "Tried to book a first service was told I would be called by service guy. Didn't happen." A separate, related pattern shows up on the parts and aftersales side: Gav Hampson (26 Jun 2025): "once they have your money the aftersales support and communication completely stops," and a fifth reviewer, Andy (27 Aug 2026), describes the same kind of unresolved chasing on a parts/aftersales matter.

What's real: Phillip McCallen Motorcycles Ltd (Companies House No. NI036668, incorporated Aug 1999, status Active) has traded under the same name for 26 years from one site at Lissue Industrial Estate West, Lisburn. Its Trustpilot profile (independently re-fetched) shows a TrustScore of 2.4/5, "Poor," from 16 reviews. A direct HTML search of phillipmccallen.com found a Magento storefront with a "Customer Login"/"My Account" area offering order and shipping status for parts purchases, and nothing beyond that: no customer-facing workshop job status tool or aftersales case tracker of any kind anywhere on the site.

What's illustrative: the sample tickets below are fictional, not real customers, bikes, or cases. Two job types are scoped here, a workshop job and an aftersales/parts issue, because those are the two distinct patterns the reviews actually describe (a service or repair that goes quiet, and a parts/aftersales query that goes quiet after payment), not a copy of an internal process this dealer has published anywhere. No staff member's name is used anywhere on this page: one review names an individual, but with only that single, uncorroborated mention, this demo uses generic department labels instead of attaching that name to fictional data.

What I'd flag before this goes further

Only the four sample reference codes above return anything. This isn't connected to a real workshop job or a real parts/aftersales case. A real build would read from whatever Phillip McCallen Motorcycles already uses to track a booked-in job or a parts/warranty query, likely a workshop diary and a parts order system, rather than a new system for staff to keep updated separately.
Automatic SMS/email pings are simulated as an internal log entry here, not actually sent. A real build wires each stage change to an SMS or email the moment staff mark it, which is exactly the gap the reviews describe: being told someone would call back and then not hearing anything.
Each ticket here carries a staff-set target completion date, and the overdue flag is a straight comparison against today's date. A real build would let Phillip McCallen Motorcycles set that date from whatever it already promises a customer at drop-off or first contact, rather than this placeholder being typed in free-form.
Two departments are scoped here on purpose: a workshop job (booking in through collection) and an aftersales/parts issue (query through resolution), because those are the two distinct complaint patterns found in the reviews, not an assumption about how the business is actually organized internally.
