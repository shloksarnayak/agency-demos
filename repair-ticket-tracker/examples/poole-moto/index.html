Working example, built for Poole Moto

Once a bike is booked in for a service, or a warranty part is promised, the only way to get an update is another phone call

Poole Moto (514 Blandford Road, Poole, Dorset) is a family-run, multi-franchise dealer established 2007, stocking over 200 bikes and scooters across around a dozen brands, with an in-house workshop open six days a week plus on-site CBT/DAS rider training. Multiple independently dated 2026 Trustpilot reviewers describe the same gap around service and aftersales communication: simon dunn (12 Oct 2026) waited roughly two hours for a booked service with no updates and no advance notice of the delay, and says a promised warranty-part confirmation email never arrived. Liam (12 Oct 2026) reports being told an importer "could not be contacted" about an issue, left unresolved. Grumpy Old Chef (26 Aug 2026) describes repeated attempts to reach the company with no response. Marcus69 (19 Aug 2026) says emails and messages about a purchase received no helpful reply.

What's real: Poole Moto's Trustpilot profile shows a TrustScore of 2.6/5, "Poor," from 60 reviews, 26 of them in the last 12 months and 63% one-star. The dealer's storefront runs on the MotoGB multi-dealer platform (per the Birdeye business listing). Its own domains (poolemoto.co.uk, poolemoto.com, and the MotoGB-hosted poolemoto.motogb.co.uk) returned HTTP 503 on every fetch attempt this session, so no customer-facing booking or status feature could be directly observed on the live site, consistent with, not proof of, what the reviews describe: finding out about a delay only by chasing the shop directly, not through any automated update.

Worth stating plainly: ratings for this dealer are mixed, not uniformly bad. Google reviews aggregated via third-party listings show a much higher average than Trustpilot, and the single most recent review found anywhere (31 Oct 2026) is positive and specifically praises a service callback, the same behavior this page assumes is missing. The complaint pattern above is real and current, but it may affect some jobs or customers rather than every one.

What I'd flag before this goes further

Only the four sample reference codes above return anything. This isn't connected to a real workshop job or a real warranty/parts case. A real build would read from whatever Poole Moto already uses to track a booked-in service or a warranty/importer query, rather than a new system for staff to keep updated separately.
Automatic SMS/email pings are simulated as an internal log entry here, not actually sent. A real build wires each stage change to an SMS or email the moment staff mark it, which is exactly the gap the reviews describe: a promised callback or confirmation email that never arrives.
Each ticket here carries a staff-set target completion date, and the overdue flag is a straight comparison against today's date. A real build would let Poole Moto set that date from whatever it already tells a customer at drop-off or first contact, rather than this placeholder being typed in free-form.
Two departments are scoped here on purpose: a workshop service job (booked-in through collection) and a warranty/aftersales issue (logged through resolution, including a case waiting on a manufacturer or importer), because those are the two distinct complaint patterns found in the reviews, not an assumption about how the business is actually organized internally.
