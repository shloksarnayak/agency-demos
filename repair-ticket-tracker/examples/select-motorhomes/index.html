Working example, built for Select Motorhomes

Once a job or a sale stops being routine, the only way to get an answer is another phone call

Select Motorhomes (Newton Abbot, Devon) runs both a sales floor and its own workshop. Across its reviews, two named staff, workshop manager Simon and service technician Dan, come up again and again, in positive reviews for being responsive and in negative ones for the opposite: a promised callback that never comes. One MotorhomeFun forum review (Dec 2024) describes a roughly 4-day repair turnaround that stretched into weeks with "no accurate information on what was actually going on," ending in the vehicle being rejected after 8 weeks. A Trustpilot review (Aug 2025) describes the same shape on a different repair: "they were never available and they always said they were ringing back then didn't." Two further Trustpilot reviews describe the same pattern on the sales side of the business, a consignment sale payment chased daily until a legal threat, and a vehicle-rejection dispute that escalated to a formal Letter Before Action.

What's real: Select Motorhomes Ltd (Companies House No. 14659493, incorporated Feb 2023) runs its workshop through a linked company, Select Motorhomes Service Centre Ltd, at the same Newton Abbot site. Its Trustpilot profile (independently re-fetched) shows a TrustScore of 4.1/5, "Great," from 35 reviews, 23% of them 1-star. The MotorhomeFun review quoted above names workshop manager Simon directly; the Trustpilot reviews name both Simon and technician Dan, in both praise and complaints. A direct crawl of selectmotorhomes.co.uk found only a vehicle-stock status flag (Featured, Reserved, Sold) attached to for-sale listings, no customer-facing job tracker or payout tracker of any kind, and no login or portal anywhere on the site.

What's illustrative: the sample tickets below are fictional, not real customers or vehicles. Two flows are scoped here, a workshop job and a consignment sale, because those are the two distinct patterns the reviews actually describe (a repair that goes quiet, and a sale-or-return payout that goes quiet), not a copy of an internal process Select Motorhomes has published anywhere.

What I'd flag before this goes further

Only the four sample reference codes above return anything. This isn't connected to a real workshop job or a real consignment sale. A real build would read from whatever Select Motorhomes and its Service Centre already use to track a repair or a sale-or-return listing, likely a workshop job sheet and a simple sales spreadsheet, rather than a new system for staff to keep updated separately.
Automatic SMS/email pings are simulated as an internal log entry here, not actually sent. A real build wires each stage change to an SMS or email the moment Simon, Dan, or sales staff mark it, which is exactly the gap the reviews describe: being told "they were ringing back" and then not hearing anything.
The workshop's "stuck" threshold here is set at 96 hours, roughly the 4-day turnaround actually promised in the MotorhomeFun review. A real build would let Select Motorhomes set its own target per job type rather than use this placeholder figure.
Two flows are scoped here on purpose: a workshop job (fault diagnosis through repair) and a consignment sale (listing through payout to the seller), because those are the two distinct complaint patterns found in the reviews, not an assumption about how the business is actually organized internally.
