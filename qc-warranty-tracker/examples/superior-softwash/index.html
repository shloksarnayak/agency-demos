Working example, built for Superior SoftWash

A documented before-and-after, and a 30-day check-in, before a dispute has to reach BBB

A BBB complaint against Superior SoftWash describes conifer damage an arborist attributed to the job, disputed in part because plant damage from cleaning chemicals can take a month or more to show up, well past a 14-day reporting window in the contract. A separate complaint describes grass and bush damage the company declined responsibility for. This isn't about who was right in either case, it's about what protects both sides next time: a timestamped before/after photo record logged the moment a job finishes, and an automatic 30-day check-in on landscaping-adjacent jobs, so a slow-developing issue gets caught and documented on a known timeline instead of surfacing as a surprise complaint months later.

What's real: Superior SoftWash is BBB A+ rated and accredited, MHIC licensed, and has no booking, scheduling, or documentation software visible on its live site today, only phone and a contact form. The complaint pattern this tool targets is drawn from two BBB complaints, one involving arborist-documented conifer damage and a disputed 14-day reporting window, the other involving grass and bush damage where the company declined responsibility, plus a separately found Checkbook.org review describing a similar landscaping-damage dispute.

What I'd be straight with you about: this tool doesn't take a side on whether any specific damage was caused by a job. It exists precisely because that's hard to determine after the fact without a record. A timestamped before/after photo and a scheduled 30-day check-in protect a business from an unfounded claim just as much as they protect a customer from a real one, that's the pitch, not an accusation.

What's illustrative: every job, customer name, and concern report below is a fictional example built to show the pattern, not a real Superior SoftWash job or customer record. Nothing here is connected to a live system.

What I'd flag before this goes anywhere near a real job

This checklist is a starting point, not your final one. It's built from the property-damage dispute pattern in your public BBB record, a real rollout should be checked against what your own crews already know to look for and photograph before and after a job.
It doesn't replace your estimate or scheduling process. This sits as a gate before "mark complete," it doesn't touch how a job gets quoted or booked.
The 30-day check-in is a placeholder cadence. A real build can use whatever window makes sense for landscaping-adjacent jobs specifically, and route the reminder to whoever owns customer follow-up.
Nothing here emails or calls a customer. It's an internal documentation and tracking tool; it doesn't change what the customer sees or send anything on your behalf.
