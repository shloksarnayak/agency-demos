Checking in on a matter without calling and waiting

A Trustpilot reviewer, Omer Mohammed, wrote about a cash house sale: "Poor communication. No response to any of my queries," with the other side's solicitors having to push for progress. Another reviewer described chasing daily on an expiring mortgage offer and being given repeated "5-7 more days" answers. A third described the firm handling an estate matter going quiet for days at a time with duplicate enquiries needed just to get a reply. A fourth put it most directly: the firm "will not return calls or emails even when I ask for senior management." All four describe the same gap: nothing tells a client where their matter stands unless they call and wait, or call again.

What's real: the quotes above are drawn directly from My Law Matters' own public Trustpilot page (716 reviews, TrustScore 4.2, 221 posted in the last 12 months; roughly 120+ of the 716 are one-star, about 17% of the total, even though 72% are five-star overall). A second, independent panel on solicitors.com aggregates 746 reviews at a 4.1 average across Google, Trustpilot, and Reviews.co.uk. A direct fetch of mylawmatters.co.uk today found a WordPress site with a "Call us now" link, an "Enquire now" contact form, and a linked Tawk.to live-chat widget, which is customer-support chat, not a case-status tool, so no client portal or matter-status lookup exists anywhere on the site today. Nationally, competitor Bird & Co already markets "a dedicated conveyancer and case-tracking software" as a selling point, confirming clients in this exact market expect this kind of visibility.

What I'd flag before you roll this out anywhere else

Nothing here sends a real email or text. This demo only shows what a client and anyone else named on the matter would receive. A real version needs an actual email/SMS service (for example SendGrid or Twilio) wired to whatever field your fee earners already update to mark a matter's stage.
This introduces visibility your site doesn't have today, it doesn't sit beside an existing tool. Your Tawk.to widget is support chat, a live conversation, not a self-serve status lookup, and today's only options are calling or using the contact form and waiting. Lookup by matter reference or email answers "where does my matter stand" the moment a client wants to know, without a login or a call.
The escalation action below directly answers the specific complaint about senior management not calling back. When a client requests a callback from the lookup screen, it creates a flagged item in the fee earner view with a committed response-by time, instead of a request that can go unanswered with no record anyone made it. This doesn't guarantee a faster resolution, only that the request itself is visible and timestamped rather than invisible until someone happens to notice a missed call.
The milestone stages shown for a conveyancing matter and for an estate matter are typical stages for that kind of work, not copied from your firm's internal case-management process. A real build should confirm the exact stages your team already tracks and the field they update to move a matter between them.
Worth a specific conversation before this goes live: what a status update can safely say. Since this touches active legal matters, a real build should keep the message content to a status label and a plain next step, as shown below, not matter substance, so nothing privileged or confidential goes out over an unencrypted text or email.
The Trustpilot and solicitors.com figures above are real and cited directly, but the overall picture is a real, partial communication gap on an otherwise-functioning firm, not a universal breakdown. 72% of Trustpilot reviews are five-star, and several reviewers praise specific named staff for being responsive once reached. What this changes is whether a client has to call and wait to find that out, not whether every matter moves instantly.
