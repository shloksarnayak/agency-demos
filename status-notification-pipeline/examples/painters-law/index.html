Working example, built for Painters Law LLP

Twenty outstanding enquiries, and no way to see which ones are still waiting

Painters Law's own Trustpilot page carries a 3.6 TrustScore across 53 reviews, sitting right on the line between "Average" and "Great," and within that record five independently named and dated reviewers, spanning roughly 16 months from March 2025 to July 2026, describe the same shape of problem: enquiries and emails that sit unanswered until the client chases. Grace (4 Oct 2025) described around 20 outstanding enquiries left with no progress, no explanations and no apologies. This example shows what that looks like fixed: every outstanding enquiry is logged and tracked on its own, not folded into one vague "in progress" label, a matter that goes quiet past a set number of days is flagged automatically, and a client can check exactly which of their questions have been answered without picking up the phone.

A direct fetch of that site's page source found no client portal, case-status page, or tracking login of any kind, and three current live job vacancies across both offices. What I'd flag before you roll this out anywhere else

Nothing here sends a real email or text. This demo only shows what a client would receive. A real version needs an actual email/SMS service, for example SendGrid or Twilio, wired to whatever system your fee earners already update to mark a matter's stage.
Each outstanding enquiry now gets its own visible record, not just a generic stage label. Below, a fee earner can log an enquiry the moment a client raises one, and mark the oldest open enquiry answered the moment a reply goes out. The client sees each one listed by name with its own open-or-answered state. Built directly from Grace's review describing around 20 outstanding enquiries left with no progress, no explanations and no apologies, and from Paul's review describing not being able to get a reply to questions without multiple attempts.
The quiet-matter flag answers Martin Wall's and Carl-Darren Marx's reviews specifically. Martin Wall described waiting weeks on end during a conveyancing matter and only ever getting a response on a Friday; Carl-Darren Marx described replies routinely taking two or three days. Every matter below shows when it was last updated, and once a matter passes the no-update threshold without contact, an "overdue for an update" flag appears automatically in the fee earner view, before a client has to call and ask. A fee earner can clear it by logging progress or by sending a plain check-in, even when nothing has actually changed yet.
This closes a visibility gap, it doesn't resolve a quality-of-service complaint. Martin Wall's review also raises a separate point about "petty enquiries" slowing his conveyancing down, a substantive handling question a tracker can't fix. Scoped honestly: this fixes "we never heard anything about where things stand," not whether the underlying enquiries themselves were reasonable.
This introduces visibility the firm's site doesn't have today, it doesn't sit beside an existing tool. A direct fetch of painterslaw.co.uk found no client portal, case tracker, or matter-status lookup anywhere on the site; the only way a client can reach the firm today is by phone or email, and wait for a reply, which is exactly the gap the reviews describe.
