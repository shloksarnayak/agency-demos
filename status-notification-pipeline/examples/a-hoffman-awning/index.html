What I'd flag before this goes further

Nothing here sends a real text or email. This demo shows exactly what a customer would receive at each step. A real version needs an actual SMS/email service (for example Twilio or SendGrid) connected to whichever calendar or job-tracking system the shop already uses.
The six stages shown (request received, appointment scheduled, parts ordered, parts in stock, installer en route, job complete) are a typical awning install/repair flow, not copied from Hoffman's internal process. Worth a short conversation before this gets built for real; a coating touch-up, a motor swap, and a full flat-pan install likely move through different steps.
The BBB complaints are real and quoted from public record; the fix is a reasonable response to the documented pattern, not a guarantee it eliminates every delay. A part can still take four months to arrive. What this changes is whether the customer hears that automatically, on a schedule, instead of finding out by calling repeatedly.
The appointment-reminder button is separate from advancing a job's stage. That mirrors the two things the original note called out: confirmations/reminders tied to the existing scheduling process, and a proactive ping during longer parts waits. They don't have to ship together.
The intake form only captures a request, it doesn't book a specific time. Keeping it to "what you need and how to reach you" is deliberate, matching phone-based intake rather than promising a live scheduling calendar this demo doesn't have.
