The letter that shouldn't sit for two months before anyone tells the client

Haleys Solicitors' own Trustpilot page is genuinely bimodal: a 3.5 out of 5 TrustScore across 18 reviews, exactly half five-star and half one-star. The one-star half repeats one shape across a 12-month span: calls and emails that go unanswered, and no proactive word from the firm while a live immigration, criminal, or probate matter sits waiting. This example shows what that looks like fixed: every matter carries a visible "last updated" marker, a matter that goes quiet past a set number of days is flagged automatically, and the moment a letter or notice comes in from the Home Office, a court, or a registry, a fee earner can log it and the client is told straight away, not two months later.

The firm itself, per its own site, handles criminal law and criminal appeals, immigration, road traffic cases, wills and probate, and challenges to restraint and freezing orders. It is a BSB-regulated entity (its SRA register entry states "we do not regulate this organisation" and names the Bar Standards Board as the actual regulator) while 12 SRA-regulated individuals also work there, an unusual but not disqualifying structure. A direct fetch of the firm's Trustpilot page this session found a 3.5 out of 5 TrustScore across 18 reviews, exactly 9 five-star and 9 one-star. What I'd flag before you roll this out anywhere else

Nothing here sends a real email or text. This demo only shows what a client would receive. A real version needs an actual email/SMS service, for example SendGrid or Twilio, wired to whatever field your fee earners already update to mark a matter's stage.
The specific gap K P described, a Home Office letter sitting for nearly two months before anyone said a word, is the one new thing this build adds. Below, a fee earner can log an incoming letter or notice the moment it's received, whatever it is: a Home Office decision, a court listing, a registry confirmation. Logging it immediately tells the client it's arrived and gives a committed follow-up window, instead of letting it sit until the client happens to call and ask.
The quiet-matter flag answers Gordy Frow's and chad palo's reviews specifically. Both describe being ignored for months despite repeatedly chasing by phone, WhatsApp, and email, not a matter that simply moved slowly. Below, every matter shows when it was last updated, and once a matter passes the no-update threshold without any contact, an "overdue for an update" flag appears automatically in the fee earner view, before a client has to call and ask. A fee earner can clear it by advancing the matter or by sending a plain check-in, even when nothing has actually changed yet.
This closes a visibility gap, it doesn't resolve a billing dispute. chad palo's and Christine's reviews both involve money, an eight-month wait and a Legal Ombudsman complaint to get a refund, and a deposit refund still outstanding six months after it was requested. A status tracker makes it impossible for a refund request to quietly sit unacknowledged, but issuing an actual refund is still a decision only Haleys can make. Scoped honestly: this fixes "we never heard anything," not "we disagree about what's owed."
This introduces visibility your site doesn't have today, it doesn't sit beside an existing tool. A direct fetch of haleys-solicitors.co.uk found no client portal, case tracker, or matter-status lookup anywhere on the site; the only way a client can reach the firm today is by phone, email, or the "Let's Chat" contact form, and wait for a reply, which is exactly the gap the reviews describe.

Worth a specific conversation before this goes live: what a status update or correspondence notice can safely say. A real build should keep message content to a status label, a plain next step, or confirmation that something arrived, as shown below, never matter substance, immigration case detail, or figures, so nothing sensitive goes out over an unencrypted text or email.
The Trustpilot score above is real and cited directly, and it is a genuinely even split, not a uniformly poor record. Of the same 18 reviews, exactly half are 5-star, several praising individual staff members by name for being responsive and supportive. What a quiet-matter flag and a log-it-the-moment-it-arrives habit change is whether a gap in communication gets caught and fixed automatically, not whether every file is currently being mishandled.
