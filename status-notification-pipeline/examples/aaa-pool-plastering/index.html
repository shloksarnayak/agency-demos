What I'd flag before you roll this out anywhere else

Nothing here sends a real email. This demo shows exactly what a customer, and your staff view, would show at each step. A real version needs an actual email service connected to whatever you use to track a job today, even if that's just a shared spreadsheet or calendar right now.
The six-stage flow (scheduled, crew on-site, drain and acid-wash complete, plaster/finish applied, curing, final walkthrough complete) is a reasonable read of how a replastering job actually proceeds, not copied from your internal process. Worth a short conversation before this gets built for real; your team may track or stage a job differently, and retiling-only or renovation jobs that don't involve plaster at all aren't modeled here.
The invoice is modeled separately from the stage flow, not as one more stage. This directly answers the home-sale reviewer's story: the job itself was finished, the invoice was the thing left hanging for two months. Below, "Final walkthrough complete" and "invoice sent" are tracked independently, so a finished job with an unsent invoice stays visibly flagged on the staff view until someone clears it, instead of disappearing once the physical work is done.
The customer can request their own invoice instead of calling and leaving a voicemail. Once a job reaches "Final walkthrough complete," the customer's own view below shows a "Request my invoice" action. That request shows up as a flag on the staff view immediately, it does not depend on anyone picking up the phone.
The "days since last update" flag uses a 14-day threshold chosen for this demo, not a number from your own process. It is a generic staleness check on any job still in progress, a direct answer to the Birdeye review's description of a crew showing up and leaving without a word, with nothing told to the customer in between visits.
Both reviews quoted above sit alongside otherwise mixed public ratings for your business (Yelp shows 2.4 stars from 8 reviews; the Yext-syndicated listing shows 3.9 from 33, with other reviews there describing fair pricing and one-day completion). This is disclosed here rather than glossed over: the communication gap described is real and recurs across more than one customer and listing, but it is not the only kind of feedback your business has received.
