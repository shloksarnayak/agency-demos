What I'd flag before you roll this out anywhere else

Nothing here sends a real text or email. This demo shows exactly what a customer would receive at each step. A real version needs an actual SMS/email service (for example Twilio or SendGrid) connected to whichever system your showroom and warehouse staff already use to mark an order's stage.
The six-stage flow shown (consultation, measured, ordered from manufacturer, received at local warehouse, installation scheduled, installed/complete) follows the order journey described on your own site. Worth a short conversation before this gets built for real since a multi-location, multi-generation business may track a signed-estimate or financing-approval step separately, and that would need its own stage.
Lookup by order number, phone, or email, no account or password. This answers "where's my kitchen" the moment a customer wants to know, without them having to remember a login or call a specific showroom.
This does not fix a wrong-size or wrong-color cabinet, and I'm not claiming it does. Two of your Yelp reviews (Gina S., Toms River: a wrong cabinet delivered twice, holding up the job 3+ weeks; U R., Toms River: a 30 inch cabinet shipped as 24 inches) describe a product/fulfillment problem a tracker alone can't prevent. What the "flag item issue" action above does is make that problem visible and tracked the moment it's found, with its own notification to the customer, instead of surfacing only when someone calls to chase it.
A single showroom screen, not five separate logins. Whichever employee at whichever of your showrooms last touched an order updates it in one place, so a status change doesn't depend on reaching the one person who originally took the order.
The Yelp and HomeAdvisor reviews quoted above are real and independently re-fetched today, but the most detailed ones (David R., Adele P., Gina S., U R.) are several years old by Yelp's own relative dating. More recent complaints exist but are less detailed, and no confirmed complaint from the last 12-24 months was found, disclosed honestly rather than presented as a current crisis.
