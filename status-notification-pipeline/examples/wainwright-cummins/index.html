Where a chaser or a complaint actually goes, instead of going quiet

Wainwright & Cummins' own Trustpilot page records at least seven separately dated reviews, spanning 2021 to 2025, describing the same pattern across different practice areas: chasers that go unanswered, a will that was paid for and never completed, a conveyancing transfer still unregistered more than a year after completion, and two formal complaints, one that took fifteen months to get a reply, one from a legal aid client that was never answered at all. This example shows what a client sees instead: a self-serve status check by matter reference, and a complaint channel that logs an acknowledgment and a committed response date the moment it is used.

What's real: Wainwright & Cummins LLP is an SRA-regulated recognised body (SRA no. 548743) with two Brixton offices, founded 1985, covering criminal defence, family law, housing, immigration, conveyancing, wills and probate, commercial work and mediation. A direct fetch of the firm's own Trustpilot page found a 3.3 out of 5 TrustScore across 38 reviews, with named reviewers Sara Bowden, Minh, F Biscuit, Ian, Lorraine, Corcuera and Allan Peter FraserKeston each describing an unanswered chaser, a stalled matter, or a complaint that went unacknowledged for months. F Biscuit's review specifically states the firm's own complaints process did not resolve the matter within eight weeks, leading the reviewer to escalate to the Legal Ombudsman. A direct fetch of wainwrightcummins.co.uk (About and Pricing pages) found no client portal, case tracker, or matter-status lookup anywhere on the site; fee quotes are handled by downloadable Word and Excel documents, phone, or email. A second, independent panel on solicitors.com shows a very different 4.9 out of 5 across 120 reviews for the same firm, a real divergence disclosed honestly below rather than picked around. Competitors Access Group, Clio, and Osprey Approach already market exactly this kind of client portal and case-tracking capability to firms of this size.

What I'd flag before you roll this out anywhere else

Nothing here sends a real email, text, or letter. This demo only shows what a client would receive. A real version needs an actual email/SMS service, for example SendGrid or Twilio, wired to whatever field your fee earners already update to mark a matter's stage.
The complaint channel directly answers the two specific reviews about complaints going unacknowledged for fifteen months, and never being answered at all. When a client raises a concern from the lookup screen, it is timestamped and logged immediately as a visible item in the fee earner view, with a committed response date shown to the client, rather than a call or email that can sit unrecorded until someone happens to notice it. The eight-week response window shown below is modelled on the complaints-handling target referenced in one of your own Trustpilot reviews, it is not confirmed as your firm's actual written complaints procedure, and a real build should use your real policy and real target dates.
The Land Registry registration stage on the conveyancing track is built directly from a specific complaint: a reviewer describing a property transfer still unregistered more than a year after completion, with emails unanswered in the meantime. Tracking registration as its own stage, separate from completion, means a client can see it is still outstanding instead of assuming the matter is finished once completion happens.
This introduces visibility your site doesn't have today, it doesn't replace or sit beside an existing tool. Your own site directs clients to leave feedback on Trustpilot, Review Solicitors, or Google, but has nowhere for a client to check a live matter status or log a concern. A real build should confirm the exact stages each practice area (conveyancing, wills, family, criminal, housing, immigration) already uses internally; the stages shown here for conveyancing, wills, and legal aid family matters are typical for that kind of work, not copied from your firm's case-management system.
Worth a specific conversation before this goes live: what a status update or complaint acknowledgment can safely say. Several of your practice areas, including legal aid family and criminal matters, involve sensitive and sometimes safeguarding-relevant content. A real build should keep message content to a status label, a plain next step, and a response date, as shown below, never case substance, so nothing privileged or sensitive goes out over an unencrypted text or email.
The Trustpilot score and the solicitors.com figure above are both real and cited directly, but they diverge sharply (3.3 out of 5 from 38 reviews versus 4.9 out of 5 from 120), and I could not independently verify the source platforms behind the solicitors.com aggregate. Twenty-six of the 38 Trustpilot reviews are four or five stars, several reviewers praise specific staff by name once reached, and the firm has no published SRA or Solicitors Disciplinary Tribunal decision against it. This reads as a real but partial communication and complaints-handling gap on an otherwise functioning, 40-year-old firm, not a universal breakdown, and what this changes is whether a chaser or a complaint is recorded and dated, not whether every matter moves instantly.
