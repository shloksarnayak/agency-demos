A regulator found two things wrong, not just one

A published Legal Ombudsman decision (14 March 2026) against Russell Evans Rahaman found both that the underlying service on a client matter was poor, and that the firm's own handling of the resulting complaint was not reasonable. That second finding, a complaint that itself went unanswered or unresolved properly, is the specific gap this example closes: a client who raises a concern about a matter gets it logged immediately, with a visible, committed response date, instead of depending on whoever happens to pick up the phone or check the inbox that day.

What I'd flag before you roll this out anywhere else

Nothing here sends a real email or letter. This demo only shows what a client would receive. A real version needs an actual email service, for example SendGrid, wired to whatever field your fee earners already update to mark a matter's stage.
The "raise a concern" channel directly answers the Legal Ombudsman's specific finding, that the firm's own complaint handling "was not reasonable." When a client raises a concern from the lookup screen, it is timestamped and logged immediately as a visible item in the fee earner view, with a committed response date shown back to the client, rather than a call or email that can sit unrecorded until someone happens to notice it. The 10-working-day window shown below is an illustrative placeholder, not a confirmed written complaints-handling target for your firm, and a real build should use your actual policy and dates.
This is one verified finding, not a demonstrated pattern, and the pitch should not overstate it. The Legal Ombudsman decision is real and formal, but every other review source checked (ReviewSolicitors, Birdeye, Yably, Justice Directory, Our Life Plan) is uniformly strongly positive. What a concern tracker and an automatic "overdue for an update" flag change is whether a future gap gets caught and dated immediately, not a claim that your clients are currently telling you this repeatedly.
Your own site already offers a Clio-hosted client login stating clients are "consistently updated," and I could not confirm whether that specific configuration already shows a live matter-stage view or a response log like this. If it does, part of this is redundant with what you already have; if it doesn't, which is common for a generic practice-management client login used mainly for secure messaging and document sharing, this adds the missing piece. Flagging this honestly rather than assuming the gap exists just because the public-facing site doesn't show it.
The underlying Legal Ombudsman matter was categorised only as "Other," not tied to a specific practice area. The sample matters below deliberately span four of your six practice areas, property, family, wills, trusts and probate, and employment, with litigation and immigration handled identically but not shown, so nothing here implies the complaint came from one particular practice area.
This closes a visibility and acknowledgment gap, it doesn't resolve a case outcome, an advice-quality judgment, or the underlying financial remedy. A concern tracker makes it impossible for a complaint to go unacknowledged or undated, but deciding what actually happened and what, if anything, is owed is still entirely the firm's own call, exactly as it was in the Legal Ombudsman's decision.
No named individual contact was found for this firm; the published contact point is a shared departmental inbox. A real build should confirm who specifically monitors incoming concerns day to day, so a raised concern cannot itself sit unseen in a general inbox, which is close to the exact pattern the Legal Ombudsman decision describes.
