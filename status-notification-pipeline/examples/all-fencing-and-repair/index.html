What I'd flag before you roll this out anywhere else

Nothing here sends a real text or email. This demo shows exactly what a customer would receive at each step. A real version needs an actual SMS/email service (for example Twilio or SendGrid) wired to whatever your office already uses to mark a permit filed, approved, or an inspection scheduled.
The six stages shown (permit submitted, permit approved, install scheduled, install complete, inspection scheduled, passed) are a typical fence-permit flow, not copied from your internal process. Worth a short call before this gets built for real — your office may track city permits by jurisdiction differently (Sunrise, Davie, Plantation, and Coral Springs don't all move the same way).
The "Mark inspection failed" button is the part built directly from your BBB complaints. Try it on the second sample job: instead of the customer having to call and re-call, they get an automatic message the moment the office flags a correction, plus a re-inspection date once it's rescheduled. That is the specific gap both complaints describe.
The BBB complaints are real and quoted directly; the fix is a reasonable response to the documented pattern, not a guarantee every inspection passes the first time. A correction still has to get made. What this changes is whether the customer hears about it automatically instead of texting the office repeatedly and getting no reply.
The "log a new job" form here is separate from your existing quote-request form. Your quote form already works for bringing in leads — this piece only starts once a job is signed and needs a permit, so it wouldn't replace anything currently working.
