A conveyancing update shouldn't only arrive after a client has already chased twice

Law and Lawyers' own Trustpilot page carries a 4.0 TrustScore, "Great," across 366 reviews, 31 of them in the last 12 months. Within that record, several reviewers from earlier in 2026 describe the same shape of problem on live residential conveyancing matters: months going by with essentially no proactive word from the firm, calls and emails left unanswered, and in one case a case handler changing three times with an eventual escalation to the Legal Ombudsman. That sits alongside an equally real set of reviewers from the same period describing fast, well communicated completions, so this looks like a gap affecting a meaningful minority of matters, not every file. This example shows what closes that specific gap: every matter carries a visible "last updated" marker, a matter that has gone quiet past a set number of days is flagged automatically for the fee earner, and a client can check status by matter reference or email without needing anyone to pick up the phone.

What I'd flag before you roll this out anywhere else

Nothing here sends a real email or text. This demo only shows what a client would receive. A real version needs an actual email/SMS service, for example SendGrid or Twilio, wired to whatever field your fee earners already update to mark a matter's stage.
The feature this firm specifically needs is the quiet-matter flag, not just a stage-change notice. The named 2026 reviews above describe going quiet for months with no proactive update, not a matter that simply moved slowly. Below, every matter shows when it was last updated, and once a matter passes the no-update threshold without any contact, an "overdue for an update" flag appears automatically in the fee earner view, before a client has to call and ask. A fee earner can clear it by advancing the matter or by sending a plain check-in, even when nothing has actually changed yet, which the base stage-advance mechanic alone cannot do.
The absence of an existing client portal is a disclosed access gap, not a confirmed negative. A direct fetch of lawandlawyers.co.uk returned almost no readable content to the available tool, so the site itself could not be fully checked. No client portal, case tracker or status login is mentioned anywhere in the firm's own Trustpilot profile, which is the strongest available evidence and what this is grounded in, but a real engagement should confirm directly with the firm whether anything exists today.
The conveyancing stages shown are typical stages for a UK residential sale or purchase, not copied from Law and Lawyers' own case-management system. A real build should confirm the exact stages your fee earners already track and the field they update to move a matter between them, office by office if that differs between London and Leeds.
Worth a specific conversation before this goes live: what a status update or check-in can safely say. A real build should keep message content to a status label and a plain next step, as shown below, never matter substance, so nothing privileged goes out over an unencrypted text or email.
The Trustpilot score above is real and cited directly, and it is a strong, mostly positive record, not a one-sided complaint pattern. 4.0 out of 5 across 366 reviews means the communication gap described above affects a minority of matters, several of them in the same period as clearly positive, named reviews praising fast replies. What a quiet-matter flag changes is whether that minority gets caught and fixed automatically, not whether every file is currently being mishandled.
