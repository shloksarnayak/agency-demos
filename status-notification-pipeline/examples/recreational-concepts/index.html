What I'd flag before you roll this out anywhere else

Nothing here sends a real email. This demo shows exactly what a customer, and your staff dashboard, would show at each step. A real version needs an actual email service (for example SendGrid or Microsoft Graph, since your mail already runs on Microsoft 365) connected to whichever system each department already uses to log a ticket.
The five-stage flow and the four department lanes (service, pools, billing, electrical) are a reasonable read of your contact page's own department structure, not copied from your internal process. Worth a short conversation with Denise, Cortney, Jennifer, Patty, and Scott before this gets built for real, your team may route or stage things differently, and your retail store and estimate-request flows aren't modeled here at all.
The 72-hour "manufacturer response promised by" flag models the Pentair review specifically, it is not a policy you've stated. It shows what an automatic overdue flag would look like on a manufacturer warranty claim, computed from when the claim was opened, so staff see it before the customer has to visit the office to ask.
The "days since last update" flag uses a 14-day threshold chosen for this demo, not a number from your own process. It is a generic staleness check: any ticket nobody has touched in a while gets surfaced to staff automatically, which is the direct answer to both the five-month liner wait and the month-long service callback described in your reviews.
The two corroborating reviews are not perfectly clean "communication-only" examples. Kirsten M.'s Yelp review pairs "worst communication" with a workmanship complaint ("their work is shoty") in the same sentence, and a related Google review (Skelly Bixler, a five-month liner wait) pairs the delay with an alleged installation defect. Disclosed here rather than glossed over, since both could partly read as quality complaints riding alongside the communication complaint.
Your overall ratings are decidedly positive. Google is 4.0 stars across 140 reviews and even Yelp's negative cluster is a minority of 11 reviews. The communication-gap pattern is real and recurring across multiple years, but it's a persistent minority theme, not the dominant customer experience, worth knowing for outreach framing, not a sign of a widespread problem.
