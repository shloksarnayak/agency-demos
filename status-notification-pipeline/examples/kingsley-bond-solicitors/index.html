The update that goes out before a client has to chase for one

Kingsley Bond's own Trustpilot page has 20 reviews; exactly 10 of them, spread across the last 11 months and as recent as last week, describe the same thing: having to repeatedly chase the firm for a case update because emails and phone messages go unanswered for extended periods, with no proactive status communication during a house sale or purchase. This example shows what that looks like fixed: every matter carries a visible "last updated" marker, a matter that has gone quiet past a set number of days is automatically flagged for the fee earner, and a client can check status by matter reference or email without needing anyone to pick up the phone.

What's real: Kingsley Bond Solicitors Limited is an SRA-regulated recognised body (SRA no. 668981, recognised since 12 August 2020) with five offices (Godstone, Alton, two in London, and Borough Green), principally residential conveyancing alongside family, commercial property, corporate, criminal, dispute resolution, insolvency and probate work. A direct fetch of the firm's Trustpilot page found a 2.7 out of 5 TrustScore across 20 reviews, with 10 separate reviewers (Alessio C., Jane Cleave, "Regular Guy," Laura Peters, "Jack," "BH," "Anna," Keith Blackburn, "Aaa Aaa" and Hadeel Alfouadi) each describing the same chasing-for-updates pattern, dated from December 2025 to October 2026. A direct fetch of kingsleybond.co.uk found a stock WordPress brochure site with no client portal, case-status login, or automated update mechanism of any kind; the only way a client can check a transaction today is by calling or emailing and waiting for a reply, which is exactly the gap the reviews describe. The same "poor communication during conveyancing" pattern was independently found at several other UK conveyancing providers checked for comparison, so this reads as a real, structural gap at this firm, not an unfair singling-out of an industry-wide problem.

What I'd flag before you roll this out anywhere else

Nothing here sends a real email or text. This demo only shows what a client would receive. A real version needs an actual email/SMS service, for example SendGrid or Twilio, wired to whatever field your fee earners already update to mark a matter's stage.
The feature this firm specifically needs is the quiet-matter flag, not just a stage-change notice. Ten of your own Trustpilot reviews describe going quiet for extended periods with no proactive update, not a matter that moved slowly. Below, every matter shows when it was last updated, and once a matter passes the no-update threshold without any contact, an "overdue for an update" flag appears automatically in the fee earner view, before a client has to call and ask. A fee earner can clear it by advancing the matter or by sending a plain check-in, even when nothing has actually changed yet, which the base stage-advance mechanic alone cannot do.
This introduces visibility your site doesn't have today, it doesn't sit beside an existing tool. A direct fetch of kingsleybond.co.uk found no client portal, case tracker, or matter-status lookup anywhere across the site; fee quotes and case queries are handled entirely by phone, email or a contact form. This closes the exact gap the reviews describe rather than adding a second way to do the same thing.
The conveyancing stages shown are typical stages for a UK residential sale or purchase, not copied from Kingsley Bond's own case-management system. A real build should confirm the exact stages your fee earners already track and the field they update to move a matter between them, office by office if that differs.
Worth a specific conversation before this goes live: what a status update or check-in can safely say. A real build should keep message content to a status label and a plain next step, as shown below, never matter substance, so nothing privileged goes out over an unencrypted text or email.
The Trustpilot score above is real and cited directly, and it is one unclaimed, self-selecting sample, not Kingsley Bond's full client base. Several of the firm's other reviews are positive and name a specific conveyancer who gave fast, clear updates, which points to the problem varying by which handler a client is assigned rather than every file at the firm being mishandled. What a quiet-matter flag changes is whether that inconsistency is caught and fixed automatically, not whether every file moves instantly.
