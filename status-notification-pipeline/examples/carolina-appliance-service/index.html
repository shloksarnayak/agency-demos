What I'd flag before you roll this out anywhere else

Nothing here sends a real text or email. This demo shows exactly what a customer would receive at each step. A real version needs an actual SMS/email service (for example Twilio or SendGrid) connected to whichever system your technicians already use to log a part as ordered or a job as in progress.
The five stages shown (request received, parts ordered, parts in stock, technician en route, complete) are a typical repair-job flow, not copied from your internal process. Worth a short conversation before this gets built for real, your team may track things differently.
The BBB and Yelp complaints are real and quoted directly, the fix is a reasonable response to the documented pattern, not a guarantee it eliminates every delay. A part still arrives late sometimes. What this changes is whether the customer hears about it automatically instead of finding out by calling repeatedly or by a technician showing up unannounced.
The intake form only captures a request, it doesn't book a specific time. Keeping it to "what's broken and how to reach you" is deliberate, matching your current phone-based intake rather than promising a live scheduling calendar this demo doesn't have.
