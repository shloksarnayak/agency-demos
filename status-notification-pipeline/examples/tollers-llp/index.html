FFixpoint Studio Templates / Status notification pipeline / Tollers LLP
Working example, built for Tollers LLP

Four clients described one gap. A regulator later confirmed a second.

Four separate, named, dated Trustpilot reviews, spanning Tollers' Corby and Northampton offices and three different matter types, each describe the same thing: calls and emails going unanswered and no proactive word from the firm while a live matter sits waiting. Separately, a published Legal Ombudsman decision against Tollers LLP found both that the underlying service was poor and that the firm's own complaint handling was not reasonable. This example closes both gaps at once: every matter carries a visible "last updated" marker that flags itself once it has gone quiet too long, and a client who raises a concern gets it logged immediately with a committed response date, across all five offices and every department, not just one case type.

What's real: Tollers LLP (SRA no. 472589, recognised body, founded 1877, Lexcel-accredited) operates five offices: the head office at 1 Waterside Way, Bedford Road, Northampton NN4 7XD; 148 Bedford Road, Kempston, Bedford MK42 8BH; 2 Exchange Court, Cottingham Road, Corby NN17 1TY; Simmonds Grant (Tollers trading under that name), 4 Mill Street, Oakham, Rutland LE15 6EA; and 6 Arlington Court, Whittle Way, Arlington Business Park, Stevenage SG1 2FS, covering private client & trusts, residential conveyancing, property litigation, employment, and personal injury. A direct check of the firm's Trustpilot page found a mixed TrustScore (roughly 3.5-3.7 out of 5 across 46-50 reviews), including four named, dated one- or two-star reviews describing the same pattern: David Turney (3 June 2026, Corby, conveyancing, "unacceptable delay and lack of customer service" on a 12-property chain that went unanswered until he left for another firm); Sam Love (17 April 2025, Northampton, "constantly chasing them for updates"); Chris Coleman (14 November 2025, a power of attorney promised in a week still not done after two, "more and more obstacles put in front of us"); and Alan (10 August 2023, a cash purchase waiting five months with no progress). Separately, a published Legal Ombudsman decision against Tollers LLP (found via the ombudsman's own decision-data table) recorded a compensation award in the GBP 1,000-4,999 band, "Poor service: Yes," and "Complaint handling reasonable: No." Honestly flagged: the firm's own website could not be directly fetched this run (502 responses and empty content across several attempts on different pages, while a genuinely nonexistent page on the same domain correctly returned 404), so no client portal or case-tracking feature was found advertised anywhere searched, including job postings and directory listings, but that is the best available signal, not a confirmed absence. A comparable Northamptonshire firm, Bird & Co Solicitors, explicitly rejects online case tracking in its own marketing ("there isn't really any such thing as online conveyancing"), suggesting this gap is real across the traditional high-street tier Tollers sits in, not something a direct competitor already offers and beats Tollers on.

What I'd flag before you roll this out anywhere else

Nothing here sends a real email or letter. This demo only shows what a client would receive. A real version needs an actual email service, for example SendGrid, wired to whatever field your fee earners already update to mark a matter's stage.
The "raise a concern" channel directly answers the Legal Ombudsman's specific finding, that Tollers' own complaint handling "was not reasonable." When a client raises a concern from the lookup screen, it is timestamped and logged immediately as a visible item in the fee earner view, with a committed response date shown back to the client, rather than a call or email that can sit unrecorded until someone happens to notice it. The 10-working-day window shown below is an illustrative placeholder, not a confirmed written complaints-handling target for your firm.
The quiet-matter flag answers Sam Love's, David Turney's, and Alan's reviews specifically. All three describe being left to chase for updates across different offices and different matter types, not a matter that simply moved slowly. Below, every matter shows when it was last updated, and once a matter passes the no-update threshold without any contact, an "overdue for an update" flag appears automatically in the fee earner view, before a client has to call and ask.
This closes a visibility and acknowledgment gap, it doesn't resolve a case outcome, legal advice, or a fee dispute. A concern tracker makes it impossible for a complaint or a stalled matter to go unacknowledged or undated, but deciding what actually happened and what, if anything, is owed is still entirely the firm's own call, exactly as it was in the Legal Ombudsman's decision.
