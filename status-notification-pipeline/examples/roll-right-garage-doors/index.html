The email that goes out the moment an installation date moves, not after a customer calls to ask

Roll Right Garage Doors' own Trustpilot page carries a strongly positive 4.3 to 4.5 TrustScore across 342 to 345 reviews, but six separate named reviewers spanning March to August 2026 describe the same gap: installation dates moving without being told, and long waits with no courtesy call to say what is happening. This example shows what that looks like fixed: every job carries a visible stage and installation date, a reschedule sends its own dated notice stating exactly how many times the date has changed, and a job that goes quiet past a set number of days is automatically flagged for the office before a customer has to chase.

What I'd flag before this goes further

Nothing here sends a real email. This demo only shows what a customer would receive. A real version needs an actual email service, for example SendGrid or Postmark, wired to whatever the office already uses to mark a job's stage and installation date.
The feature this business specifically needs is a dated reschedule notice, not just a stage-change notice. The reviews above describe installation dates slipping without explanation, more than once on the same job, until the customer cannot tell whether the install is still happening at all. Below, rescheduling a job's installation date sends its own notice stating the new date and exactly how many times that job's date has changed, separate from the stage-advance message, so a customer never has to work it out themselves.
The quiet-job flag answers the other half of the pattern: long waits with no proactive word. Every job shows when it was last updated, and once a job passes the no-update threshold without any contact, an "overdue for an update" flag appears automatically in the office view, before a customer has to call and ask. The office can clear it by advancing the job, rescheduling it, or sending a plain check-in even when nothing has actually changed yet.
The four stages shown (survey booked, door manufactured, installation scheduled, installed and complete) match the research's own description of this business's process, they are not copied from Roll Right's actual internal system. A real build should confirm the exact stages the office tracks and whatever field marks a job's progress today.
This introduces visibility the site doesn't have today, it doesn't replace an existing tool. A direct fetch of rollrightltd.co.uk found no customer-facing order or installation status page anywhere on the site; the only account-adjacent feature is a warranty-registration form. This closes the exact gap the reviews describe rather than adding a second way to do the same thing.
The Trustpilot score above is real and cited directly, and it is a strongly positive 4.3 to 4.5 across 342 to 345 reviews, not a struggling business. The six reviews quoted are a real minority thread within a mostly positive record, including one reviewer (Janet) who was happy with the finished door despite the communication complaint. This tool addresses visibility during the process, it is not a claim about the quality of Roll Right's finished installations.
