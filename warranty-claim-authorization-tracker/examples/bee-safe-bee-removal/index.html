The warranty is already real. This makes it visible.

Bee Safe Bee Removal's own service page markets a job warranty as a core selling point: "we not only do bee removals but also warranty our work," and its own customer-reviews page quotes customers describing "a one year guarantee." But the company's BBB complaint record shows that promise breaking down in practice once a customer needs it. One complaint describes a customer who paid $774 under the one-year warranty, had bees return, and despite calling, texting, and emailing multiple times over weeks, could not get the company to come finish the job. A second, similarly shaped complaint describes bees returning within weeks of a paid removal, with the customer reporting "no communication for more than a month" while trying to use the warranty. A third disputes a surprise $350 "extermination" charge added after the company killed bees without authorization, partially refunded to a net $370 but never reversed on the charge itself. Nothing on the customer's side today lets them see whether a return visit is scheduled, confirmed, or being ignored, and nothing stops an add-on charge from being billed before the customer has agreed to it. This page shows one small, working tool that fixes both: every job's one-year warranty window is visible from a reference number, a claim opens a status ticket with a stated response-time commitment instead of a phone call the customer has to keep repeating, and any charge added beyond the original warranty scope is itemized and held until the customer explicitly acknowledges it, before it is ever billed.

Everything below is fictional. All customer names and claim details on this page are sample data, clearly labeled as such, and not Bee Safe Bee Removal's real records. The claim types shown (bees returning within warranty, a return visit finding a separate new nest) reflect the kinds of issues named in Bee Safe Bee Removal's own BBB complaint record, not any specific real customer's file. A real build reads from whatever Bee Safe Bee Removal's office already uses to log a job and connects the response-time and charge-authorization prompts to real email or text.

How this gets built for real

The reference number can be whatever Bee Safe Bee Removal already gives a customer. A real build reads from an existing job or invoice number, it does not invent a new one to remember.
The one-year warranty window is set the moment a removal job is completed. A real build can text or email that window to the customer automatically, closing the exact gap the BBB record describes: a customer with no way to check whether they are still covered short of calling in.
The response-time commitment is a real clock, not a promise. A claim that sits at "Claim received" past its stated response window shows as overdue in the office view, the direct fix for the "no communication for more than a month" and "twelve contact attempts" shape described in the BBB record.
An itemized charge genuinely cannot be billed without customer acknowledgment. If a return visit finds a separate problem beyond the original warranted removal, staff can itemize that charge, but it is held until the customer checks a box and types their name from their own status page, the direct fix for the disputed $350 "extermination" charge that was added without authorization.
"Acknowledged" here is a typed name and a timestamp. A real build can add an actual e-signature provider for a stronger record; this demo shows the workflow, not a specific vendor.
