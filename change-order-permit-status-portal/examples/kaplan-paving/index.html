FFixpoint Studio Change-order, permit & schedule portal / Kaplan Paving example
Independent work sample, built for Kaplan Paving, LLC

No scope change, permit result, or schedule date reaches the customer late again

Kaplan's own BBB responses describe two separate complaints as a "communication lapse" and a "communication breakdown": a driveway dimension was changed and a card charged with no customer sign-off first, and a failed village permit inspection reached the customer only through the village's own enforcement notice, not from Kaplan. A third pattern shows up on ComplaintsBoard.com: "over a month of leaving voicemails" just to find out a signed job was actually on the schedule. This page shows one small, working tool that closes all three gaps: a change order that blocks the crew and the card charge until the customer explicitly confirms it, a permit-inspection result that pushes to the customer the moment it's logged, and a schedule confirmation that fires the moment a job is actually put on the calendar.

Everything below is fictional. All customer names, jobs, dollar amounts and addresses are sample data, clearly labeled as such, and not Kaplan's real records. A real build reads from whatever Kaplan's office already uses to track signed contracts (their existing WordPress/Enhancify intake) and connects the notification pings to real email or text.

How this gets built for real

The reference number is whatever Kaplan already gives a customer. A real build reads from the signed contract or invoice number already in Kaplan's WordPress/Enhancify intake, it doesn't invent a new number to remember.
The notification pings here are log entries, not real sends. A real build wires the "on the calendar," "permit result," and "change order needs your confirmation" pings to Kaplan's existing email so they go out the moment office staff log the event, not after a callback.
"Confirmed" here is a typed name and a timestamp. A real build can add an actual e-signature provider if Kaplan wants a stronger legal record for scope/price changes; this demo shows the workflow, not a specific vendor.
The card-charge block is illustrative. A real build ties this same gate to whichever payment processor Kaplan's "PAY INVOICE" link already uses, so a change-order charge simply cannot fire before the customer has confirmed it.
