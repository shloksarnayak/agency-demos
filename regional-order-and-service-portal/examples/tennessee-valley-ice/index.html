Tennessee Valley Ice: one portal for six locations, three request types

Tennessee Valley Ice runs packaged ice, dry ice, construction and special-event ice delivery, plus ice-machine sales and repair, across six locations in Tennessee, Georgia and North Carolina. Every service line on their live site funnels through the same single "Request a Quote" callback form, with no online ordering, account, or status tracking anywhere, and posted office hours of Mon-Fri 8:00am-4:30pm with no self-service option outside those hours. This example shows a customer picking their nearest location, then submitting a standing recurring order, a same-day emergency or event request, or an ice-machine service ticket, each routed straight to that location's own queue.

Sample account data below is fictional. Locations, products, and posted hours are real, independently verified from Tennessee Valley Ice's own site (tennesseevalleyice.com, fetched this run). Customer names, contact details, and order/ticket history shown are invented and clearly labeled as such.

How this gets built for real

Grounded in what's actually published. The six locations, product lines, and Mon-Fri 8:00am-4:30pm hours here are copied directly from Tennessee Valley Ice's own live site (homepage, /ice-machine-services, /contact, independently fetched). Exact per-location addresses and any real per-location routing logic beyond location name would come from the business directly.
Intake and routing, not full dispatch or billing. This stays narrow on purpose: request intake, hub routing, a shared queue, and the same-day/after-hours flag. Route planning, driver dispatch, invoicing and payment are separate scope from what's live on their site today, and would layer on top of this, not replace it.
Why this over just adding online ordering. The gap isn't only "no online ordering", it's that emergency ice and special-event ice, the two most time-pressured product lines, have zero self-service path outside 8:00am-4:30pm Monday to Friday. A generic order form fixes the ordering gap; this fixes the hours gap too by letting a request be logged and queued the moment it happens, even overnight or on a weekend.
