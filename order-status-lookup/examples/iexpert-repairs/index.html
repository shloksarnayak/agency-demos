A status check for the customer who can't just walk in

Your reviews praise the personal touch, technicians calling customers directly rather than a chatbot. That works well for a walk-in repair. For your UK-wide mail-in service, a customer who posted their phone in has no way to check where it stands between calls, other than WhatsApp or the contact form. A nearby competitor, Gadget Club in Witham, markets automated SMS and email updates at every step as a selling point. Here's what that could look like for a mail-in repair, without replacing the calls that make your service what it is.

What's real: your live site and contact page, checked directly today, show no automated status-tracking layer for mail-in repairs, WooCommerce, WhatsApp click-to-chat, and a contact form, but nothing that pushes a status update on its own. Gadget Club's own site genuinely markets automated SMS/email notifications as a feature, a real, named comparison point, not a hypothetical.

What I'd be straight with you about: this isn't built around a service complaint, your reviews are strong and specifically praise your communication. This is a structural gap, not a fix for something broken: a mail-in customer, unlike a walk-in one, can't just look over the counter to see if their device is ready. An automated status update doesn't replace the phone call you make when something needs a decision, it just covers the ordinary "is it done yet" question in between.

What's illustrative: the two sample repair references below aren't real jobs. They show what typing a reference into a working version of this page would return, using a flow a mail-in repair typically goes through. A real build would connect to however your team currently logs an incoming device.

What I'd flag before you roll this out anywhere else

Only the two sample repair references above return anything. This isn't connected to your real intake system, so it can't look up an actual customer's device. A real version would need a link to however your team currently logs a device when it arrives.
The four stages shown (received, diagnosed, repair in progress, dispatched) are a typical mail-in repair flow, not copied from your internal process. Worth a short conversation about how your team actually phases a job, especially the "we call you if the fault differs" step, before this gets built for real.
This is a status check, not a replacement for the calls you already make. Anything that needs a customer decision, like a fault differing from what was booked, should stay a phone call. This only covers the routine "where's my device" question.
This only shows status. It doesn't quote a repair or take payment, it just means a mail-in customer isn't left guessing between drop-off and the call that tells them it's ready.
