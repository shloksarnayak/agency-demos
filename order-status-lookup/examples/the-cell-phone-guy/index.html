A cost estimate and status text, the moment the diagnosis is done

Your site has a contact form for questions, but nothing that tells a customer what a repair will cost or where it stands once a device is dropped off. BBB and Birdeye complaints, spanning different devices and different years, independently describe the same gap: no cost estimate, no status update, the customer has to call in to find out anything at all. Here's what an automatic text at each step could look like.

What's real: your live site has no cost-estimate tool, status tracker, or booking system anywhere, checked directly today, only a contact form. Complaints on BBB and Birdeye, across separate incidents and separate years, describe the same specific pattern: a repair dropped off with no pre-authorization estimate given, and a status check that only happens if the customer calls in first. One review describes going three weeks with no update at all.

What I'd be straight with you about: a handful of complaints across BBB and Birdeye aren't proof this happens on every repair, most drop-offs presumably go fine. What's notable is that the same specific gap, no estimate before work starts, no update once it's underway, shows up independently across different devices and different years rather than in one bad week. A status text doesn't replace the phone call for something that needs a real conversation, it just means the ordinary "what's this going to cost, is it ready yet" question gets answered without one.

What I'd flag before you roll this out anywhere else

Only the two sample repair references above return anything. This isn't connected to your real intake system, so it can't look up an actual customer's device. A real version would need a link to however your team currently logs a device when it comes in.
The four stages shown (received, diagnosed with cost, repair in progress, ready for pickup) are a typical repair-shop flow, not copied from your internal process. Worth a short conversation about how your team actually phases a job before this gets built for real.
This is a status and estimate check, not an authorization system. A customer would still need to approve a cost before work starts on anything not covered by a flat-rate quote, this only means they get asked instead of left wondering.
This only shows status. It doesn't change how a repair gets diagnosed or priced, it just means "what's it going to cost" and "is it ready" get answered without a phone call.
