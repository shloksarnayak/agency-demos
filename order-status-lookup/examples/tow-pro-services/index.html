A tow ETA the customer doesn't have to call in for

Your site has a "Request Help" form and a location finder, but once a truck is actually dispatched, there's no way for the customer to see where it stands without calling. A dated string of complaints, from BBB filings to Yelp reviews, describes the same gap: no proactive update, so the customer is the one who has to keep checking in. Here's what an automatic dispatch-confirmation and ETA text could look like.

What's real: your live site has no SMS tracking, live chat, or dispatch-status tool anywhere, checked directly today. Between January 2024 and January 2025, BBB shows five separate complaints, each describing the same root problem in different words: a promised callback that never came on a lost-keys claim, a 3:00 PM delivery that was still "an hour away" when the customer called to check, and a truck dispatched without the confirmation the customer had asked for.

What I'd be straight with you about: five complaints across thirteen months isn't proof this happens on every call, most dispatches presumably go fine. What the dated spread does show is a recurring pattern rather than one bad week, and it lines up with what Yelp reviewers and your own team describe independently (Indeed reviews for the dispatcher role mention a "pressured, under-systematized" environment). Three separate sources landing on the same specific gap, no proactive status update, is what makes this worth building rather than a single complaint would be.

What's illustrative: the two sample dispatches below aren't real jobs. They show what typing a dispatch reference into a working version of this page would return, using stages a tow call typically goes through. A real build would connect to whatever your dispatchers already use to log a call, scoped to the Nashville flagship first rather than all seven branded locations at once.

What I'd flag before you roll this out anywhere else

Only the two sample dispatch references above return anything. This isn't connected to your real dispatch log, so it can't look up an actual call. A real version would need a link to however your dispatchers currently record a job, even if that's just the board they already call off of.
The stages shown (dispatch confirmed, truck en route with ETA, on scene, complete) are a typical tow-call flow, not copied from your internal process. Worth a short conversation about how your dispatchers actually phase a call before this gets built for real.
This is a status check, not a booking or dispatch system. It doesn't replace a dispatcher deciding where a truck goes, it just means the customer gets an automatic text the moment a truck is confirmed, instead of having to call and ask.
This only shows status. It doesn't change how a call gets answered or handled, it just means a routine "where's my truck" check doesn't have to become a phone call your team has to pick up.
