A repair request shouldn't need a BBB complaint to get a status

Peerless Fence's own BBB complaints page records a recurring pattern across its nine locations: a customer reports a repair or warranty problem, and calls or emails about it go unanswered or unreturned for weeks, with no way to check where the request stands short of calling back and re-explaining it, until some customers escalate to the BBB itself. Peerless Fence's own site today offers a phone number and a contact form, and a linked Help Center subdomain that fails to load, nothing a customer can check themselves. Below is a working tool sized to close that specific gap: every reported repair or warranty issue gets a ticket that moves through Received, Scheduled, In progress and Resolved, the next scheduled visit date is shown the moment one is booked, and any ticket sitting too long with no visit scheduled is flagged automatically instead of waiting for a customer to call back or file a complaint.

Everything below is fictional sample data. Customer names, tickets and dates on this page are illustrative only, not Peerless Fence's real customers or records. Two sample tickets below are deliberately shaped to mirror the no-visit-scheduled pattern described in Peerless Fence's own public BBB complaints, to make the "needs attention" flag concrete, not because they reflect a real Peerless Fence customer or file. How this maps to Peerless Fence specifically

The reference number is whatever Peerless Fence already uses, if anything, or a simple new one handed out at intake. No login or account is required; a customer only needs the number from their original call or email.
Sample ticket PF-2201 and PF-2204 model the documented pattern. Peerless Fence's own BBB complaints describe calls and emails about a repair or warranty issue going unanswered for weeks with no stated timeline. Those two sample tickets show exactly that: reported, no visit scheduled, and the "needs attention" threshold closing without anyone having booked a date, the way the real complaints describe, instead of going quiet.
The one thing a customer actually asked for, a date, is shown the moment it exists. As soon as a ticket moves to Scheduled or In progress, the next visit date appears on both the team queue and the customer's own status check, no callback required to get it.
This sits behind the existing phone and email intake, not instead of it. Peerless Fence's own site already takes a call or a message through info@peerlessfence.com; this tool starts the clock and gives a reference number the moment that happens, rather than replacing the contact method itself.
Location is shown because Peerless Fence runs nine locations across three states. A real build would read location from whichever branch actually took the call, matching Peerless Fence's own listed Saint Charles, Round Lake, Melrose Park, Bloomington, West Chicago, Warrenville, Greenwood, Cincinnati and Delaware locations, so a multi-branch team sees one shared queue instead of nine separate ones.
The status update here is a log entry, not a real send. A real build wires it to Peerless Fence's existing Google Workspace email so the update actually reaches the customer the moment a visit gets scheduled.
Deliberately out of scope: the installation or repair work itself. Peerless Fence's own BBB responses show the underlying repairs usually do get resolved once a visit is actually scheduled. This tool only covers the communication and visibility gap before that point, where its own site has no equivalent today.
