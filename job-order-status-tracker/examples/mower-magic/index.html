A mower in for service or an order on backorder shouldn't go quiet while a customer waits to hear

Right now, a customer with a mower in for service or an order on backorder has one way to check where it stands: call the showroom or wait to be contacted, and recent Trustpilot reviews describe that update not arriving. The pattern repeats across more than one review this year: a ride-on mower paid for and collected for service, then held for weeks with parts delays and no clear status, still not returned at the time of review; a backorder that was never updated while the customer waited on it. Mower Magic's own website could not be fetched directly this session (it blocked automated requests), and nothing in its public Trustpilot business description or review content mentions an order or repair status page, a customer account, or automatic progress updates, consistent with those reviews describing silence rather than a tracker that failed. Below is a working tool sized to close that specific gap: every paid order or repair gets a ticket that moves through Received, Diagnosed, Awaiting parts, In progress and Ready/Dispatched, a simulated status update fires on each change, and any paid ticket stalled too long at a stage is flagged automatically instead of waiting for the customer to chase.

Everything below is fictional. Customer names, order and repair details and dates on this page are sample data, illustrative only, not Mower Magic's real customer or order records. One sample ticket below is deliberately shaped to mirror the stalled-with-no-update pattern described across Trustpilot reviews, to make the "needs attention" flag concrete, not because it reflects a real Mower Magic customer. This tool is scoped to order and repair status visibility only; it does not address the warranty or damage-related disputes some reviews separately describe, which the company contests in its own replies with specific counter-explanations, and makes no claim about resolving those.

How this gets built for real

The reference number is whatever Mower Magic already hands out. A real build reads from an existing order number or job-sheet number instead of inventing a new one for the customer to remember.
The status update here is a log entry, not a real send. A real build wires this to the business's existing email or SMS, so the update actually reaches the customer the moment a stage changes, instead of the customer having to call the showroom to find out.
This does not replace the order desk's or workshop's own judgment. It only makes an order or repair that has gone quiet, waiting on parts or a workshop slot, visible to the whole team automatically, instead of surfacing only when a customer calls to chase or leaves a review.
Scoped narrowly on purpose. This tool covers order and repair status visibility only. It does not touch warranty-eligibility or accidental-damage disputes; those need a different resolution process and are out of scope here.
