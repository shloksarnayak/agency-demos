Right now, checking on a made-to-order gown means calling and hoping someone picks up

At least three separate 2025 Trustpilot reviewers describe the same gap: a deposit goes down, the gown goes into production with a designer, and the only way to find out what is happening is to keep calling or emailing. Megan Owens (1-star, 24 Aug 2025) ordered her dress in March 2024, was told it would arrive after Christmas, heard nothing for months despite "chasing repeatedly," and said the dress finally arrived in the wrong size: "The communication from Verona Bridal was awful." Emma (3-star, 15 Aug 2025) had her dress arrive in late April instead of the promised December or January, with emails unanswered, and wrote "Some communication from Verona Bridal would have been of benefit." Ciara Fitzgerald (1-star, 11 May 2025) paid extra for a specific material that never arrived and said "The shop will ignore you like the plague once you've paid them." I checked veronabridal.co.uk directly: it runs on a generic Shopify storefront with no order-tracking, status-lookup, or client-portal feature anywhere in its navigation or footer, and its Delivery & Returns page gives no production timeline and no promise of status updates. Below is a working tool sized to close that specific gap, not the whole business: a bride enters her order reference and sees exactly where her gown stands from deposit to collection, and the boutique gets one shared queue instead of a phone line everyone is chasing.

What I'd flag before this goes further

The seven stages shown (deposit paid, gown ordered from designer, in production, shipped to boutique, arrived and ready for fitting, alterations booked, collected) are a typical made-to-order bridal workflow, not copied from Verona Bridal's own internal process. Its real internal stage names, and whether its designer partners (Berta, Pnina Tornai, MillaNova, Steven Khalil, Jenny Yoo, Moira Hughes) each report production progress differently, would take a short conversation to confirm before this gets built for real.
The order reference here is invented for this demo. A real build reads from whatever order number the boutique already writes on its own paperwork at deposit, rather than inventing a new one for the bride to remember.
The status update here is a log entry, not a real send. A real build wires this to Verona Bridal's existing email or phone system so an update actually reaches the bride the moment a stage changes, which is the exact gap all three reviews above describe: going quiet for months after a deposit, with brides left to chase repeatedly.
This does not shorten how long a made-to-order gown actually takes a designer to produce. It means the bride and the boutique both see where an order stands, on request, before it becomes months of unanswered calls and emails, the same gap behind every review quoted above.
