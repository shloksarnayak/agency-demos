Working example, built around ECH Group's own published facts

A rescheduled or missed installation date should reach the customer before they have to chase it

ECH Group's own Trustpilot reviews, spanning multiple months in 2026, repeat the same specific gap: an installation or repair visit gets rescheduled, delayed or missed, and the customer finds out by chasing rather than being told first. A direct fetch of echgroup.co.uk's own site found no customer-facing portal, order-status page or tracking login today, only a quote form and a general contact form. Below is a working tool sized to close exactly that gap: every job gets a ticket that moves through Survey booked, Design/DNO approval, Installation scheduled and Installed/commissioned, a simulated status email fires automatically whenever the stage changes or the installation date itself is set or moved, and the whole team sees the same shared queue instead of one person's diary.

Everything below is fictional. Customer names, job references and dates on this page are sample data, illustrative only, not ECH Group's real customer or job records. One sample job is deliberately shaped to show what an installation-date change and its automatic notification look like, not because it reflects a real ECH Group customer. This tool is scoped to job-stage and installation-date visibility only; it says nothing about ECH Group's overall installation quality, which its own Trustpilot TrustScore (over 4.0, "Great," from several hundred reviews) shows is generally strong, this is a narrow fix for the specific scheduling-communication pattern described in the reviews, not a claim about a wider problem.

How this gets built for real

The reference number is whatever ECH Group already hands out. A real build reads from an existing job or quote number instead of inventing a new one for the customer to remember.
The status email here is a log entry, not a real send. A real build wires this to the business's existing email system so the update actually reaches the customer the moment a stage or an installation date changes, instead of the customer finding out when the fitters do not turn up.
The installation-date change is the point. Every time a date is set or moved, the customer gets told automatically as part of the same step, not as a separate task someone has to remember to do.
This does not replace the survey or install team's own judgment. It only makes a job that has gone quiet, or a date that has moved, visible to the whole team and the customer automatically, instead of surfacing only when the customer calls to chase or leaves a review.
