Open an account without a card number written on paper

Some B2B rental, wholesale and trade businesses still open a house account the same way they did twenty years ago: a customer downloads a credit-application PDF, fills it out by hand, and faxes or emails it back, often alongside a second form asking them to write out a full card number and CVV code. This page shows an online replacement for that whole flow using a fictional rental company.

Every business name, rate and submitted record below is fictional sample data, generated for "Sample Rental Co." When this gets built for a real business, the equipment list and rates come from what they actually rent and charge, the way the example below shows.

How this gets built for real

The card number never gets stored, not even in this demo. Only the last four digits and expiry are kept, the way a real tokenized processor works: the full number goes straight to something like Stripe or Authorize.net's own hosted field, and the business's own systems (and this page's own code) never see or store it complete.
No CVV is collected here at all. A live build never routes a CVV through the business's own server or database either, it goes directly from the customer's browser to the processor.
The charge authorization is specific, not blanket. The customer sees and sets an actual dollar cap for loss or damage charges, instead of pre-authorizing an open-ended percentage of retail value with no further notice.
"Signed" here is a typed name and a timestamp. A real build can add a certified e-signature provider if the business wants a stronger legal record; this demo shows the workflow, not a specific vendor integration.
See it built for a real business: Equipment Rental Service, Inc., a West Palm Beach, FL heavy-equipment rental company.
