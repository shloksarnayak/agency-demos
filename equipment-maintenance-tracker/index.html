Every unit tracked against its own maintenance interval, not memory

Many dealers and rental/service businesses that sell or maintain equipment (tractors, mowers, compressors, pumps, and similar machinery) track required maintenance and break-in intervals informally, if at all, with no shared reminder system and no way for a customer to check status without calling in. This page shows one small, working alternative: every sold or serviced unit is tracked against its own hours- or calendar-based maintenance interval, gets flagged automatically before it's overdue, and a customer can check a unit's status and any open service ticket on their own.

Everything below is fictional. When this gets built for a real business, the interval types and thresholds match how that business actually schedules maintenance, and the team board reads from whatever already tracks sold and serviced units.

How this gets built for real

The meter reading here is entered by hand. A real build pulls it from telematics where the manufacturer supports it, or from a simple entry at every service-desk visit.
The warning buffer is one shared percentage here. A real build sets it per equipment type, since different machinery has different break-in and service tolerances.
The service ticket update here is a log entry, not a real send. A real build wires it to the business's existing phone, text, or email system so the customer actually hears about each update instead of calling in to ask.
