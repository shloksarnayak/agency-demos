The rules underneath

These are the part worth arguing with.

01
WhenAn emergency message describes a symptom without the details that distinguish a simple fix from a refrigerant or compressor issue, compressor noise, frost buildup, seal condition, current temperature.
ThenThe system asks a short, fixed set of questions before dispatch is confirmed, rather than sending a tech out on a bare symptom.
The review this page is modeled on describes exactly this gap, a phone diagnosis without enough detail led to the wrong first visit and a second emergency call to actually find the freon leak. A short intake step catches that before a truck rolls, it doesn't ask the customer to diagnose it themselves.
02
WhenThe intake answers point at something beyond a routine fix, refrigerant symptoms, an unusual noise pattern, anything a phone triage script isn't confident about.
ThenThe ticket still goes to a person to decide what to send the tech out with, the system gathers, it never diagnoses.
A wrong diagnosis costs more than the few minutes an intake question takes, it costs a second emergency visit and the customer's confidence in the response-time claim your marketing is built on.
03
WhenA request is a direct lookup, confirming a scheduled visit, with no symptom or diagnosis involved.
ThenThe system answers immediately and sends without waiting for a person.
Keeping routine confirmations instant is what makes the intake step above worth having, it only adds friction where friction actually protects the fix.

What this demo can't do

The parts a proposal would normally leave out.

This is a hypothesis worth testing, not a proven pattern. The evidence here is one homepage testimonial about your speed and one separate review about a misdiagnosis, not a documented volume of missed calls. Worth confirming with your own team how often a first visit misses the real issue before building this for real.
The integration is the product. A real version needs to actually route the intake answers to whoever schedules dispatch, and ideally to the tech's ticket, not just collect them. Without that, this collapses into an extra email nobody reads before rolling a truck.
This doesn't replace a tech's judgment on-site. It only makes sure the tech shows up with more than a bare symptom description, the diagnosis itself still happens in person.
This only routes and asks three questions, it never decides what's wrong on its own. Keeping that with a person or the tech on-site is deliberate.
