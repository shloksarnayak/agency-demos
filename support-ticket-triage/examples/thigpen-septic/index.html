The rules underneath

These are the part worth arguing with.

01
WhenA second or later message arrives about the same job with no reply in between, by text, call, or email.
ThenUrgency moves up, and the queue label counts the attempt number so nobody has to open it to know what it is.
The two dated reviews this page is modeled on both describe repeat contact going unanswered, not a single missed message. Counting the attempt is what catches that before it becomes a third try, or a review.
02
WhenThe reply would require deciding whether a charge was fair or a recurring issue needs a different fix.
ThenThe system gathers the job reference and contact history, drafts a reply, and holds it for a person who's actually seen the work to send.
A 52-year reputation isn't protected by a fast reply, it's protected by a correct one. Guessing at a billing or diagnosis decision in an automated message would risk more than the delay it's trying to avoid.
03
WhenA request is a direct lookup against something already scheduled, with no dispute attached.
ThenThe system answers immediately and sends without waiting for a person.
This is the request type easiest to let slide on a busy day precisely because it sounds routine, and it's the same shape of request the real reviews describe going unanswered. Closing it automatically frees a person for the two above.

What this demo can't do

The parts a proposal would normally leave out.

The integration is the product. A real version needs read access to your scheduling and invoicing to confirm an appointment or check a work order. Without that, all three replies collapse into a polite acknowledgment.
Less than 3% of your ~440 reviews are negative. This isn't a claim your team is unresponsive, it's built around a specific, narrow gap, follow-up after an issue is open, that showed up in two recent, dated reviews. Most jobs presumably go fine without ever needing this.
Which line and inbox this watches is your call. Your site lists two phone numbers and one email, plus a contact form. Triage pointed at the wrong one is worthless, worth a five-minute conversation before this gets built.
This only routes and drafts, it never decides a billing dispute or a repair diagnosis on its own. Keeping that judgment call with a person, always, is deliberate.
