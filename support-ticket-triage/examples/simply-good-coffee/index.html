The rules underneath

These are the part worth arguing with.

01
WhenA message mentions a prior failed phone or chat attempt, especially one involving your automated system.
ThenFlag it as a second contact attempt even though it arrived as a first written message, since the customer already tried once.
Multiple BBB complaints this year describe the same shape: a customer calls, hits the automated system, gets nowhere, then has to find another way in. Treating the written message as a fresh, first-time contact undercounts how many times this customer has already tried.
02
WhenThe reply would require diagnosing a hardware issue or deciding a warranty outcome.
ThenThe system gathers what it can, the product, the issue, the prior contact history, drafts a reply, and holds it for a person to actually diagnose before sending.
A five-year warranty program is worth something only if a real diagnosis happens before a decision gets made. Guessing at a fix in an automated reply would be worse than the delay it's trying to avoid.
03
WhenA request is a direct lookup, has an order shipped, with no dispute or diagnosis attached.
ThenThe system answers immediately and sends without waiting for a person.
This is exactly the kind of request that shouldn't need a human at all, and one of the real complaints this page is modeled on describes a customer who couldn't even get this simple a question answered because the automated phone system mishandled their order number. A written channel with a reliable direct-lookup answer is a lower bar to clear than fixing the phone system, and it closes the same gap.

What this demo can't do

The parts a proposal would normally leave out.

This is a written-channel fix, not a phone-system fix. The complaints I read describe your AI phone system itself as the obstacle, garbled order numbers, being transferred between automated branches. This demo addresses what happens once a customer gives up on the phone and writes in instead, it doesn't change the phone system itself. That's a separate, harder conversation.
The integration is the product. A real version needs read access to your Shopify order data to answer a shipping question correctly, or to pull up a customer's warranty and order history for a hardware issue. Without that, both replies collapse into a polite acknowledgment.
I don't know your current call and email volume. These two tickets are grounded in complaints from this year, they aren't a claim about how often this happens across all your orders. Whether this is worth building depends on numbers only you have.
This only routes and drafts, it never diagnoses a hardware issue or decides a warranty outcome on its own. Keeping that judgment call with a person, always, is deliberate.
