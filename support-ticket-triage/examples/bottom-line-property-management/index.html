The rules underneath

These are the part worth arguing with.

01
WhenA ticket has already had one prior dispatch that didn't resolve it, especially anything involving water, shared walls, or a floor/unit above or below.
ThenIt's held for a person to check adjacent units or systems before a third technician is sent, and the queue label counts the dispatch number so nobody has to reread the history to know.
The January 2026 BBB complaint describes exactly this: repeated technician visits before anyone checked whether the source was a different unit entirely. Counting prior dispatches is what catches that before it becomes a third failed visit, or a complaint.
02
WhenThe intake form is missing a clear location, a clear symptom, or urgency isn't answered.
ThenThe system asks one follow-up question before a ticket is created at all, rather than logging a vague ticket and letting a technician discover the gap on-site.
Indeed reviews from your own maintenance staff describe the department as disorganized, with tenants showing up in person. A ticket that's missing the unit number or the actual symptom is the raw material for that disorganization; catching it at intake is cheaper than catching it on a truck.
03
WhenA request has one clear symptom, one clear unit and fixture, and no prior failed dispatch attached.
ThenThe system opens the PropertyWare ticket and routes it to the matching trade immediately, no person needs to re-triage it.
Most requests are this simple. Automating the clean cases is what frees a person's attention for the repeat leak and the missing-detail ticket, the two shapes of request that actually generate complaints.

What this demo can't do

The parts a proposal would normally leave out.

The PropertyWare integration is the product. A real version needs write access to PropertyWare to actually open a ticket and read access to see whether a unit has an open or recently-closed ticket already. Without that connection, this collapses into a nicer-looking form.
This is built around one specific, documented gap. One BBB complaint and a handful of Indeed reviews are the evidence, not a claim that most maintenance requests go wrong. Most presumably get handled fine; this targets the pattern where they don't.
Which trades exist, how they're dispatched, and your actual escalation thresholds are your call. The "when" conditions above are a starting guess, worth a short conversation before this gets built for real.
This only structures and routes, it never decides whether a leak is this unit's responsibility or the one above's. That determination still needs someone to physically check. The system's job is making sure that check happens before the third technician, not after.
