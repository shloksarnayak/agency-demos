The rules underneath

These are the part worth arguing with.

01
WhenA second (or later) message arrives from the same patient with no reply in between, on any channel, phone, email, or the website form.
ThenUrgency moves up, the queue label counts the attempt number, and it jumps ahead of newer messages.
Both real complaints this page is modeled on describe more than one unanswered attempt before the patient gave up and filed with BBB. Counting repeat contact across channels, not just within one inbox, is what catches a patient who tried the phone, then email, then the website form.
02
WhenThe reply would require deciding whether a balance, claim, or dispute is correct.
ThenThe system gathers what it can, prior contact attempts, the account reference, what's being disputed, drafts a reply, and holds it for a person to check against the actual claim and payer record before sending.
A billing dispute is exactly the kind of judgment call that shouldn't be answered by a fast, confident-sounding message that turns out to be wrong. The system's value here is making sure the draft and the missed-call history reach a person quickly, not deciding the account is right.
03
WhenA request is a direct lookup against something already on file, a receipt, a payment date, a mailing address, with no dispute attached.
ThenThe system answers immediately and sends without waiting for a person.
This is the request type that seems too simple to cause a complaint, and one of the two real BBB filings this page is built from was exactly this: a patient trying three different channels just to get a receipt. Requests with no judgment call attached are the ones automation can safely close on its own, freeing a person's time for the ones that actually need it.

What this demo can't do

The parts a proposal would normally leave out.

The integration is the product. A real version needs read access to your account and payment records to answer a receipt request correctly, or to pull up what a payer actually processed on a disputed balance. Without that, both replies collapse into a polite acknowledgment.
I don't know your current call and email volume. These two tickets are grounded in your two most recent BBB complaints, both from early 2024, they aren't a claim about how often this happens generally. Whether this is worth building depends on how many messages a week actually go unanswered, and only you have that number.
Which inbox and which line this watches is your call. Your contact page lists a primary and an alternate phone number, an email address, a fax, a website contact form, and a bill-pay portal. Triage pointed at the wrong one of those is worthless, so that's a five-minute conversation before anything gets built.
This only routes and drafts, it never decides a disputed balance on its own. Keeping that judgment call with a person, always, is deliberate, not a current limitation to be removed later.
