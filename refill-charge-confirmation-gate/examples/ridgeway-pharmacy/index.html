Ridgeway Pharmacy: confirm the price before the vial fills, not after the card is charged

Public complaint records for Ridgeway's mail-order line describe the same pattern from several different customers, on different platforms, across several years: a card on file gets charged, a shipment arrives, and the patient says they never saw the amount or the day supply coming, sometimes because it changed cycle to cycle, sometimes because it was their first fill. Because a dispensed prescription legally cannot be returned to stock, the refund is refused every time, not out of bad faith, but because federal and state pharmacy rules leave no other option once the vial has shipped. This example shows a small tool that closes the gap before it opens: a hold-and-confirm step between the fill queue and the card charge, so a cycle that changes never posts without an explicit yes.

This is not a business in crisis. All 6 of Ridgeway's BBB complaints in the 3-year window are marked Answered, its BBB rating is A-, and on the one dispute a card issuer actually adjudicated, the chargeback was decided in Ridgeway's favor, evidence that a signed authorization does exist on file. The gap is narrower and more specific: there is no per-cycle, pre-charge confirmation step between the stored card and the fill, and once a prescription ships, federal guidance and state law (for example Virginia Code § 54.1-3411.1) generally bar returning it to stock, so every one of these disputes is unfixable at the refund stage by design. Optum's and Amazon Pharmacy's own published home-delivery practices both confirm the expected shipment and amount before it goes out, which is the baseline Ridgeway's own mail-order patients are already comparing it against.

Refill pre-charge queue

Every upcoming refill, with what was charged last cycle next to what this cycle would charge. A cycle that differs, or has no prior cycle to compare against, is held and flagged instead of moving to the charge queue on its own.

Pre-charge gate check

Today's batch of charges about to reach Authorize.net, checked against what each patient actually confirmed. This is the exact check that would have caught the $267-vs-$99 complaint and the first-time 3-month-supply complaint before either card was charged.

Audit log

Every notice, confirmation, cancellation, and skip, timestamped as it happens, the record Jennifer P.'s two unanswered cancellation emails never had.

Confirm, cancel, or skip a refill

Try RW-2203, RW-2204 or RW-2205 from the pharmacy view's sample queue. RW-2201 and RW-2202 have not had a notice sent yet, so they will not be found here until the pharmacy view sends one.

How this gets built for real

The notice is sent from the pharmacy view here. A real build sends a text or email automatically the moment CyberScript's fill queue produces a cycle that differs from the patient's last charge, or has no prior cycle to compare against.
The pre-charge gate check sits between the fill queue and Authorize.net. It does not replace CyberScript or Authorize.net, and it is a different thing from AIS's own RxCall notification product: RxCall can tell a patient a shipment is coming, it does not hold the charge until the patient affirmatively responds.
Confirming, canceling, or skipping is logged the moment it happens. A real build keeps this in the same audit trail a signed intake form would produce, closing the exact gap in Jennifer P.'s complaint: a cancellation request with no record anyone acted on it.
