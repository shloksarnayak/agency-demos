The same phone line answers a true lockout and a routine key copy, before anyone knows which one it is

Your drive-thru window already splits jobs into two lanes: quick key copies and pickups that don't need a store visit, versus everything else. But every inbound call, a locked-out emergency at 9pm or a routine fob-programming question at 10am, funnels through the same single phone line with no way to tell them apart until someone answers. This is a working example of a triage step in front of that line: two taps sort a request into "get a tech moving now" or "book the drive-thru window," before it ever waits on hold.

What's real: the drive-thru window, the (919) 778-6600 line, the 8am-9pm hours, and the after-5:30pm mobile emergency service are copied from easternlocksmithservices.com and its /contact-us/ page today. What's simulated: no call or text actually routes anywhere in this demo, and the "Illustrative" figures and sample requests below are invented to show the shape of the data, not real customers or real jobs.

What I'd flag before this touches a real phone line

Nothing here actually rings a phone or sends a text. A real version sits in front of your existing (919) 778-6600 line, either as an IVR prompt or a text-back after a missed ring, so the two-question sort happens automatically instead of on this page.
It doesn't replace a person answering the phone. It just labels the call before someone picks up, so a true lockout doesn't wait behind a routine fob question, or the other way around.
Hours and the drive-thru window are copied from easternlocksmithservices.com today. Regular hours 8am-9pm, drive-thru Mon-Fri 8am-5:30pm, mobile emergency service after that and on weekends, if any of that changes this needs a one-line update to match.
The front-desk queue here is a static illustration. A real version would land wherever the desk already looks, a shared inbox or a simple board, not a separate tool to remember to check.
