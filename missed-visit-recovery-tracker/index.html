A missed visit doesn't have to end in a billing dispute

Field-service software marks a visit as a no-show, rescheduled by the technician, or not completed, and then nothing else happens on its own: no message to the customer, no internal follow-up, until the customer calls to ask why no one showed or why billing kept running. This page shows one small, working tool that fixes that. The moment a visit is marked missed, the customer gets a self-serve rebooking link, an SLA-timed recovery task opens for the office team so it can't get lost in the shuffle, and a rollup shows exactly which routes and technicians are generating repeat exceptions instead of that pattern only surfacing years later in public reviews.

Everything below is fictional. When this gets built for a real business, the trigger is whatever field-service or scheduling system already records a skipped, rescheduled, or not-completed visit (for example PestPac, ServiceTitan, or Jobber), the rebooking message goes out through a real SMS/email service instead of a log entry, and the SLA window and route/technician labels are set to match how that business actually runs.

How this gets built for real

The trigger is a real status change, not a button. A real build watches the field-service system's own record of a skipped, rescheduled, or not-completed visit and fires automatically, no one has to remember to click anything.
The rebooking message and SLA are real, not simulated. A real build sends an actual text or email with a working self-serve rebooking link, and can page or text the office once a recovery task crosses its SLA window instead of relying on someone opening this page.
The reference code is whatever the business already hands out. A real build reads from an existing job or account number instead of inventing a new one for the customer to remember.
The route and technician labels match reality. A real build uses the business's own route names and staff, and the rollup can feed straight into a weekly ops review.
