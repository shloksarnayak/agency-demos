Worked example for a real, researched business

When a technician doesn't show, the customer shouldn't have to find out by still getting billed

Corky's Pest Control has served Southern California for decades and states on its own site that it serves more than 30,000 customers. Public reviews and a BBB complaint describe a recurring pattern: a technician no-shows with no proactive reschedule, and in separate cases billing keeps running after a customer asks to cancel. This example shows the office side of a small tool that catches a missed visit the moment it is marked in the field-service system, gets the customer a rebooking link right away, and keeps the office from losing track of it.

Why this, specifically, for Corky's Pest Control

The problem is documented, not assumed. A BBB review (May 2026) describes being "no showed twice in a row with no explanation" and no callback to reschedule. A BBB complaint (July 2025) describes being stood up for scheduled service. Separate reviews on ContactUs.com describe a technician attempting or charging for service after a cancellation request, and being charged monthly for a property that was not serviced.
It extends what's already running, it does not replace it. A current job posting on corkyspest.com confirms the office uses a field-service system (PestPac) to track technician progress and update schedules. This tool watches for a skipped, rescheduled, or not-completed status in a system like that and acts on it, rather than asking the office to adopt new scheduling software.
The customer service representative role is confirmed as phone-based today. Corky's own current job posting for a Customer Service Representative lists "schedule appointments and reschedule missed services" among phone-handled duties, which matches the pattern in the reviews above: follow-up after a miss depends on someone remembering to call.
This is a small, scoped addition, not a platform switch. One integration point (the status change), one outbound notification, one internal recovery queue, and one rollup view. It sits well inside the usual custom-build range for a company of this size, and could plausibly pay for itself by preventing even a handful of the cancellation and billing complaints documented above.
