What I'd flag before this goes anywhere near a real job

The 3-visit / 21-day stall threshold and 14-day refund threshold are placeholders. A real build uses whatever your office actually considers a normal repair timeline for a luxury-brand part order, which is likely longer than a mainstream appliance but shorter than the 7-month outlier in your complaint record.
This doesn't replace your intake, dispatch, or billing process. It's an alert layer that would sit alongside however jobs, parts orders, and invoices are tracked today across your NY, Baltimore, and DC metro offices, not a new system of record.
The invoice block only catches one specific mistake. Billing a job that was promised as no-charge-until-fixed before the repair is actually done, the exact shape of the $220 complaint. It doesn't touch pricing, estimates, or payment processing.
The complaint pattern is partly a training issue this can't fix. A misdiagnosis from "a Google search of an error code" is a technician-quality problem. This tool only closes the gap where the wrong-parts, broken-promise, or slow-refund pattern goes unnoticed by the office until a customer escalates.
