Template, not built for a specific business

Turn a "he said, she said" move into three tracked records

Moving and relocation companies that coordinate crews by phone and estimate visually tend to accumulate the same three disputes: a job that runs long with no objective record of when it actually started and finished, a high-value item that gets damaged with no proof it was flagged for extra coverage beforehand, and a complaint or claim that sits unanswered until the customer escalates publicly. This page shows three small, working tools that make each of those a tracked record instead of a phone argument.

Everything below is fictional. When this gets built for a real business, it reads from whatever already takes quotes and schedules crews, a booking form, a dispatch board, and adds these three checks on top, without replacing how the work itself gets done.

1. Move-day timeline log

Crews (or office staff) log the four stage timestamps for a job, an estimated-hours figure is captured up front, and any job that runs over its estimate is flagged automatically instead of surfacing only when the customer disputes the final bill.

2. High-value item intake flag

Every quote-request inventory list is scanned for high-value keywords. A match blocks the quote from being marked ready until someone confirms extra valuation coverage was actually offered, so a $40,000 item never travels on standard released-value coverage by accident.

3. Same-day acknowledgment draft

The moment a complaint, claim, or negative review is logged, an acknowledgment draft is generated immediately, so a customer never sits for weeks with no response while the company decides who should call back.

How this gets built for real

The timestamp entry is manual here. A real build can be a mobile-friendly page crews open on their own phone at each stage, with the time stamped automatically rather than typed in.
The keyword scan is a simple word match. A real build ties the keyword list to the company's actual valuation-coverage policy and its own quote form, so it fires at the moment a customer submits, not after.
The acknowledgment is a draft shown on screen, never sent. A real build wires it to the company's own email or SMS provider so it goes out automatically, with a human still reviewing anything beyond the first acknowledgment.
