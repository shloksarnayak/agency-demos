Hunter Ashley Sales & Lettings: give every open deposit-release case a target date the office can see is overdue before a landlord or tenant has to chase it

Six separately dated Trustpilot reviewers, spanning January 2022 to July 2026, describe the same pattern once a tenancy ends or a management relationship changes hands: a deposit does not come back, and the only way to find out why is repeated unanswered calls and emails. One reviewer says a deposit from a previous property is still unreturned years on. A landlord says deposit monies were never transferred across after the agency's management of the property ended. A tenant says a deposit has been outstanding for months after a tenancy that ended on a specific date, despite repeated promised payments that never arrived. Another says a deposit has been held since redecoration was completed exactly as requested. The agency's Zoopla and OnTheMarket listing pages show only a phone number and an email contact form, no client portal or status page of any kind, consistent with every one of the 24 Trustpilot reviews describing phone or email as the only channel. Below is a small working tool sized to close exactly that gap: every open deposit-release case gets a target release date the office can see is overdue or due soon before anyone has to call twice, and a no-login reference-code lookup so a landlord or tenant can check status themselves.

How this gets built for real

A target release date is attached to the case itself, not to one person's memory. Every named point of contact across 24 reviews, in both the positive and negative ones, is the same single owner-operator; a real build keeps the date on the case record so a release does not depend on one person remembering it between other work.
A case stays visible from the day a tenancy ends until the deposit actually clears, not just at move-out. Several reviews describe a deposit still outstanding months after the tenancy ended, or never transferred after a management handover; a real build keeps the case open and overdue-flagged the whole way through, not just at the start.
Not every delay is purely internal, and this tool is built to say so. The one negative review with a company reply shows a release genuinely waiting on a previous landlord's approval; a real build lets a case be logged as "waiting on a third party" so the office and the landlord or tenant both see the real blocker, rather than every delay reading as the agency's own fault.
Overdue and due-soon cases surface automatically, before a landlord or tenant has to call or email twice. This is where a real build would notify the owner directly once a case crosses overdue, instead of relying on someone opening this page.
The reference code is whatever the firm's own tenancy or case reference already is. A real build reads from an existing file number instead of asking a landlord or tenant to remember a new code.
The landlord/tenant view shows only safe wording. Internal notes typed in the office view never appear on the lookup page.
