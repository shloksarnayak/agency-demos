Acorn Blinds And Shutters: give every booked fitting slot a status a customer can check, instead of a silence they have to chase

The company's own Trustpilot page carries three independent, dated complaints spanning 2022 to 2026 that describe the same pattern: a fitting appointment gets booked after a deposit is paid, then it slips, with no advance notice. One review describes more than five booked appointments as no-shows with the blinds never installed. Another describes a blackout-blind order where the blinds themselves went in fine, but a paired awning install dragged on for months with missed appointments, unanswered emails, and the remote control still outstanding. The company's own acornblinds.ie site has a "Book A Free Appointment" button and an online payment page, but no appointment-confirmation or order-status feature anywhere. This is consistent with a documented industry-wide pattern, not just this one business: Ireland's Competition and Consumer Protection Commission reported in 2025 that 54% of home-improvement complainants had difficulty getting in touch with the trader. Below is a small working tool sized to close exactly that gap.

How this gets built for real

A booked fitting slot gets confirmed automatically, not left to a sticky note. The moment a slot is logged here, the office and the customer both have the same record; there is no separate step where "fully booked" quietly bumps a date without the customer ever being told.
A job that looks done is not closed until every piece of it is. The remote-control-still-outstanding pattern in the reviews happens because the main blind install reads as "finished" while a small paired item is forgotten; a real build keeps a job open until every logged piece, including accessories, is marked delivered.
At-risk flagging happens before the appointment slips, not after a customer calls to ask. Once a due date crosses into the "due soon" window, this is where a real build would text or email the fitter and the office, instead of relying on someone opening this page.
The reference code is whatever the office already writes down. A real build reads from an existing order or job reference instead of asking a customer to remember a new code.
The customer view shows only customer-safe wording. Internal notes typed in the office view never appear on the lookup page.
