4US Moving: a place for every damage claim update to live until it's sent

Public complaint records for 4US Moving describe a specific, narrow pattern: a claims manager promises daily or 15-day follow-up on a damage claim, and the customer is then left calling and emailing repeatedly with no update. That is a communication gap, not a service failure, and it is fixable without adding headcount. This example shows a small tool that gives every open claim a next-check-in date the office can't lose track of, plus a customer-facing status page so a customer can check in without picking up the phone.

4US Moving operates full-service moves across Los Angeles, Orange, Riverside, San Bernardino and San Diego Counties plus the Bay Area, and advertises "transparent pricing" as a core promise. The gap this tool targets sits downstream of the move itself: once a damage claim is opened, the commitment made to the customer (a callback every day, or a decision within 15 days) has nowhere to live except a claims manager's memory or inbox. This tool gives that commitment a due date the office can see at a glance, and lets the office send a short "still working on it" update on a set cadence — even before there is real news — instead of the customer having to chase.

How this gets built for real

The claim reference is whatever the office already issues. A real build reads from the existing claim or move-order number instead of asking a customer to remember a new code.
"Send scheduled update" is a live text or email in production. Here it just logs a note and resets the countdown; a real build fires an actual message to the customer on the cadence the claims manager set, even when there is nothing new to report.
This does not replace the claims manager's judgment. It only makes sure the commitment they already made (daily, every 3 days, every 15 days) shows up before it is missed, and protects reviews by keeping the customer informed either way.
