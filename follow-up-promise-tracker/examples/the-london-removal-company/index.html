The London Removal Company: a place for every post-move claim to live until someone actually answers it

Reviews of The London Removal Company describe the same shape more than once: something is damaged or goes missing during a move, the company says it will be sorted through insurance or a callback, and then repeated calls and emails go unanswered. One reviewer put it directly: "despite repeated calls and emails, no one ever followed up." This example shows a small tool that gives every post-move claim or open enquiry a due date, an automatic overdue flag, and a place a customer or a rep can check without having to re-explain the whole thing from scratch.

The pattern shows up across more than one review: a customer reports an item damaged or an item missing after a move, is told it will be handled through insurance or a follow-up call, and then hears nothing further despite repeated attempts to reach the company. One review describes the move itself going well but the post-move claim being ignored entirely; a separate review describes a garden table damaged and an item forgotten, with no response to any subsequent emails, texts, or calls. The company's own site runs on WordPress with a bespoke quote tool for new enquiries, but shows no ticketing system, claims tracker, or customer portal for anything after a booking is made, consistent with what these reviews describe.

How this gets built for real

This sits beside the existing quote tool, not instead of it. A real build tracks what happens after a booking or a claim is opened, it does not replace how new enquiries or quotes get submitted.
The reference code is whatever the office already writes down. A real build reads from an existing job or claim number instead of inventing a new one, so nothing has to be re-keyed.
The status lookup shows only customer-safe wording. Internal notes typed in the office view stay in the office view, so a rep can check a claim without exposing internal handling notes to the customer.
