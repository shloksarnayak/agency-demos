Irish Roofers Dublin: a place for every promised return visit, callback, or invoice request to live until it's kept

Irish Roofers Dublin's own Google Business reviews describe a repeated shape underneath an otherwise strong 4.9-star record: a return visit, a callback, or an invoice is promised, and it then depends on one staff member remembering to follow through rather than on any shared record. The company's own site has a quote-request contact form only, no scheduling portal, customer account, or job-tracking login anywhere. This example shows a small tool that gives every open promise a due time, an automatic overdue flag sorted worst-first for the office across three locations, and a no-login reference-code lookup so a customer can check status without starting the call over.

The company's own testimonials page shows follow-up working well when one particular staffer happens to chase it personally, which is exactly the kind of thing a shared queue makes reliable instead of personality-dependent. Checked directly against the company's own site: no service, portal, or job-tracking page exists anywhere on it today, and everything runs through one shared phone line and a contact form.

How this gets built for real

This replaces nothing, it fills a real gap. The company's own site has no service, portal, or job-tracking page today, so a real build gives all three offices their first shared view of open promises instead of competing with an existing system.
The reference code is whatever the office already writes down. A real build reads from an existing job or quote reference instead of inventing a new one.
The status lookup shows only customer-safe wording. Internal notes typed in the office view stay in the office view, so staff can look up a call without exposing internal handling notes.
