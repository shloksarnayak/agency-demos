DPP Law Solicitors LLP: give every new enquiry a promised callback date the office can see is overdue, before the client has to chase it

The firm's own public Trustpilot replies admit this pattern twice rather than dispute it: "We manage an exceptionally high volume of cases and receive a significant amount of enquiries daily" and a commitment to "refine our internal processes to ensure no enquiry is left unanswered in the future." Four independently dated 2026 reviews describe the same gap in practice: a promised callback that never came, follow-up calls to the office going unanswered, a client who sent requested documents and then heard nothing after several follow-up emails, and a client who wrote twice asking for advice with no reply either time. The firm's own site has no client-tracking portal or case-status page of any kind. Below is a small working tool sized to close exactly that gap: every promised callback or follow-up is logged with a due time, the office queue surfaces the most overdue ones first, and a client can check status by reference code without calling the office into a silence.

How this gets built for real

A promised callback date is logged the moment it is made, not held in one person's memory. The "promised callback never came" pattern across the firm's own reviews happens when a commitment exists only in a phone call or a sticky note; a real build captures it at the point of contact so it cannot simply be forgotten.
An enquiry stays visible once documents are sent in, not just at first contact. The Hayley Bateman-shaped pattern in the reviews, documents sent and then silence despite several follow-up emails, happens when a matter is treated as handled after the first call; a real build keeps a document follow-up open until the firm actually responds.
Overdue promises surface to the office automatically, before a client calls to ask twice. Once a due date passes, this is where a real build would flag the fee-earner or intake team directly, instead of relying on someone opening this page.
The reference code is whatever the office already uses. A real build reads from an existing enquiry or matter number instead of asking a client to remember a new code.
The client view shows only client-safe wording. Internal notes typed in the office view never appear on the lookup page.
